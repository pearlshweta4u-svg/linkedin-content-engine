## Patterns (small sample: hypotheses to test, not conclusions)
1. **Real people beat designs.** The two posts with 160+ likes are candid family photos with no text on the image. Every designed or text-heavy image is under 40 likes.
2. **Emotional family stories beat clinical content**, even though her audience is professional. The father post also drew the most comments (29).
3. **Her strongest hooks open on a scene or a quote** ("The arm around my shoulder lasted 5 seconds…"). Headline-style hooks in Unicode bold did less well.
4. The clinical client story (2 Nov) did worst. This matches Shweta's feedback that client stories need to read as stories, not lab reports.
5. Timing: her LinkedIn audience skews IT/tech [ME]; all 7 posts went out between 8:00 and 10:00 IST.

## Implications for the engine
- Monday/Tuesday: use **her real photos** (family, training, clients with consent), not designed cards. This needs a photo source; she drops photos/videos into a shared Drive folder weekly [ME].
- Keep designed cards for save-worthy (Thursday) and data (Friday) posts, where the image carries information.
- Avoid Unicode bold "headline" text in posts (screen readers can't read it, and it didn't outperform).
- She has used comment-keyword CTAs ("comment IRON"); LinkedIn treats these as engagement bait, so the engine doesn't use them unless she asks.
- Topic overlap: the 11 Nov sample (ferritin) is 9 days after her 2 Nov ferritin post.
