## Q1. Were there ICMR, ICMR-NIN, FSSAI, MoHFW, Anemia Mukt Bharat or NIDDCP announcements or reports in the window?

### Takeaway
I found **no official ICMR, ICMR-NIN, FSSAI, MoHFW/PIB, AMB or NIDDCP announcement or report dated 10 Oct to 9 Nov 2026**. The main policy story in the window is a public debate in The Hindu editorials of 27 Oct and 5 Nov 2026 and in The South First over the government's plan to use **double-fortified salt (DFS, iodine plus iron)** against anaemia. This followed the February 2026 pause of fortified rice. Separately, India still has no new national anaemia figure: NFHS-6 (29 May 2026) dropped the anaemia indicators, and the DABS-I survey that is meant to replace them has not been released.

### Cited Findings
**In window: the DFS debate (CANDIDATE 1)**
- The Hindu ran editorials or opinion pieces on 27 Oct 2026 and on 5 Nov 2026. The 5 Nov piece was titled "Anaemia burden: iron alone is no solution." Their argument: anaemia has many causes, iron is not risk-free, and salt is the wrong carrier when India wants people to eat less salt. I could only see them through UPSC digests, not the original. — [Legacy IAS digest, 5 Nov 2026](https://www.legacyias.com/editorials-opinions-analysis-for-upsc-05-november-2026/); [Legacy IAS digest, 27 Oct 2026](https://www.legacyias.com/editorials-opinions-analysis-for-upsc-27-october-2026/); [Civilsdaily summary](https://www.civilsdaily.com/news/anaemia-burden-iron-alone-is-no-solution/); [Vajiram editorial analysis, 27 Oct 2026](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/daily-editorial-analysis-27-october-2026/)
- Context from the editorial coverage:
  - Anaemia Mukt Bharat was relaunched in June 2026 as Anaemia Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan with a 7x7x7 framework.
  - The Ministries of Women and Child Development and of Education direct DFS supply through PM POSHAN and Anganwadi services under Mission Poshan 2.0.
  - DFS reaches about 76 million people at about ₹14–₹20 per person per year; another source says about 80 million, so the figures conflict.
  - The authors call slow uptake "an institutional, not a technical, problem."
  - Source: [Insights IAS, 28 Oct 2026](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2026/10/28/upsc-current-affairs-28-october-2026/); [Vajiram, 27 Oct 2026](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/daily-editorial-analysis-27-october-2026/)
- The government paused the fortified-rice programme in February 2026 and has not formally named a replacement. Experts are criticising the proposed DFS plan in particular:
  - **Ignores causes:** "any strategy that ignores the root causes will not deliver."
  - **Wrong doses for the neediest:** toddlers and pregnant women eat less salt, so those who most need iron get the smallest doses.
  - **Clashes with salt reduction:** Indians already eat about 10 g of salt a day, double the WHO limit of 5 g.
  - **Evidence cited:** a 2022 Cochrane review found DFS "probably reduced anaemia prevalence by around 21 per cent compared with iodised salt," with modest haemoglobin gains.
  - Source: [The South First, "Ignored causes…" (exact date not visible; search tool described it as from the past week or so)](https://thesouthfirst.com/health/ignored-causes-why-are-experts-riled-up-over-indias-double-fortified-salt-plan-to-fight-anaemia/); [Chennai Online](https://www.chennaionline.com/news/indias-double-fortified-salt-plan-against-anaemia-sparks-debate/)
- The 5 Nov op-ed reportedly says that testing haemoglobin alone cannot find the cause of anaemia, and that ferritin, B12 and inflammation markers are rarely tested. It also cites CNNS 2016–18: iron deficiency explained only part of anaemia in children and adolescents. — [Legacy IAS digest, 5 Nov 2026](https://www.legacyias.com/editorials-opinions-analysis-for-upsc-05-november-2026/); [Ajmal IAS summary](https://ajmaliasacademy.in/anaemia-burden-iron-alone-is-no-solution/)

**Just before the window (label as older)**
- **AMB Abhiyaan, 28 Jun and 11 Aug 2026 (PIB):**
  - A 28 Jun 2026 PIB notice announced the move from Anemia Mukt Bharat to Anemia Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan.
  - The 7X7X7 framework includes women of reproductive age (20–49 years).
  - IV iron (FCM and iron sucrose) is added for pregnant and lactating women with severe anaemia or who do not respond.
  - Hb records for pregnant women go through the JANANI portal.
  - An 11 Aug 2026 PIB release covers putting the guidelines into practice.
  - Source: [PIB PRID 2279050](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2279050&reg=48&lang=1); [PIB PRID 2278584](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2278584&reg=48&lang=1); [PIB PRID 2297512](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2297512&reg=48&lang=1)
- **NFHS-6 national fact sheet (29 May 2026):** contains no anaemia data. All seven anaemia indicators were dropped (43 indicators dropped, 13 added). MoHFW said anaemia will be measured through DABS-I (Diet and Biomarkers Survey in India). — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/health/anaemia-imr-mmr-clean-fuel-use-nfhs-6-drops-key-health-indicators-126060101726_1.html); [Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1095497/india-has-no-data-on-anaemic-women-for-the-first-time-in-three-decades)
- **DABS-I (ICMR-NIN):** analysis is still under way, with "no date for completion of the analysis, submission of the final report or public release." Article date not visible. — [The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/health/india-has-no-new-anaemia-figures-the-icmr-survey-meant-to-provide-them-is-still-being-analysed/)
- **FSSAI labelling (notified 24 Mar 2026, in force 1 Jul 2027):** the Labelling and Display First Amendment Regulations 2026 require the fortification logo and a "Fortified with" declaration. This is not new in the window. — [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/consumer-protection/indian-fssais-new-labelling-rules-2026)
- **ICMR/CCRAS Ayurvedic anaemia trial (around May 2026):** a phase III trial in about 4,000 non-pregnant women aged 18–49. It reported Ayurvedic formulations "therapeutically comparable" to IFA for moderate iron-deficiency anaemia. It was presented at the ICMR Clinical Trial Meet, and I have not seen it peer-reviewed. — [Telangana Today](https://telanganatoday.com/icmr-study-finds-ayurvedic-formulations-effective-in-treating-anaemia)

### Inferences
- **Candidate 1 for a post, the DFS debate:** this is the most timely and credible "new" item for a coach. Practical angle: "A low Hb reading does not tell you why you are low. Iron is only one cause; B12, folate, heavy periods, worms and inflammation are others. Ask for ferritin, B12 and CBC indices, not just Hb. Don't add salt to 'get iron'."
- Evidence strength: this is policy commentary from expert op-eds, not new data. The only quantitative evidence is the 2022 Cochrane estimate.
- **"India has no fresh anaemia number"** is also a valid, if slightly older, hook: NFHS-6 dropped anaemia in May 2026 and DABS-I is still pending.

### Gaps

- I found no PIB release from 10 Oct to 9 Nov 2026 on anaemia, iodine or fortification. The pib.gov.in site search was not reachable.
- The South First article dates could not be confirmed.

## Q2. Were there notable Indian peer-reviewed studies (IJEM, Lancet regional, IJMR) in the window on these topics?

### Takeaway
I found **no peer-reviewed Indian study first published online in the 10 Oct to 9 Nov window** on these topics. The closest is **ICMR-SALT-T 1** in *The Lancet Regional Health – Southeast Asia*. It was published online on **17 Sep 2026**, which is before the window, and assigned to the **November 2026 issue (vol 54, article 100866)**. It is strong and highly relevant to thyroid health. Present it as "recent (Sept 2026)", not as news from the window.

### Cited Findings
**CANDIDATE 2: ICMR-SALT-T 1** (Marwaha, Thomas, Jha et al.)
- Source details: *Lancet Reg Health SE Asia* 54:100866; online 17 Sep 2026; issue Nov 2026. — [Lancet RH-SEA full text](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lansea/article/PIIS2772-3682(26)00151-4/fulltext); [Medical Dialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/mdtv/diabetes-endocrinology/videos/salt-iodisation-cuts-goitre-in-indian-children-but-thyroid-autoimmunity-is-rising-lancet-study-179731); [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/health/4-decades-of-salt-iodisation-cut-goitre-in-indian-kids-but-thyroid-autoimmunity-rising-lancet-study/3047879/); [The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/health/goitre-down-thyroid-autoimmunity-up-what-four-decades-of-iodised-salt-show-in-indian-children/)
- **Design:** cross-sectional study of 8,903 schoolchildren aged 10–18 in eight regions, Jan 2024 to Dec 2025.
- **Iodine status:** median urinary iodine 133.2 µg/L, which is adequate by WHO criteria.
- **Goitre:** 23.5% in 2003 down to 10.3%.
- **Thyroid autoimmunity:** anti-TPO positive in about 10.4% of the 5,726 children tested. Sources quote 10.3% or 10.4%, so check the paper. The figure was about 4.4% two decades after salt iodisation began.
- **Subclinical hypothyroidism:** about 13%.
- **Limits:** the study cannot show that iodine caused the rise in autoimmunity. The authors recommend keeping universal salt iodisation, with quality control and longitudinal follow-up.
- This study is of children, not adult women. Any extrapolation to women aged 30–50 is an inference.

**Other 2026 items (older than the window)**
- **IJEM, around Jun 2026, rural Tamil Nadu:**
  - Sample: 630 rural adults.
  - Iodine status: more than a quarter were biochemically iodine-deficient (median urinary iodine below 100 µg/L).
  - Salt coverage: Tamil Nadu has India's lowest household coverage of adequately iodised salt, at 61.9%.
  - Source: [Medical Dialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/diabetes-endocrinology/news/iodine-deficiency-persists-in-rural-communities-despite-salt-iodization-suggests-study-177789)
- **ICMR-SPIC, Lancet RH-SEA vol 52, published 31 Jul 2026** (DOI 10.1016/j.lansea.2026.100825):
  - Stillbirths: 3,595 (1.7%) in the sample.
  - Link to anaemia: 2,091 of these (58.2%) were among women with moderate-to-severe anaemia.
  - Severe anaemia: risk ratio of about 3.12.
  - Source: [Lancet RH-SEA](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lansea/article/PIIS2772-3682(26)00110-1/fulltext); [LSHTM repository](https://researchonline.lshtm.ac.uk/id/eprint/4683012/)
- **IJEM Jul–Aug 2026 issue, thyroid nodules study:**
  - Sample: mean age 37.3 years; 75% female.
  - Findings: 52.5% were vitamin D deficient, and 95% had normal TSH.
  - Source: from search summary only, so it needs verification. [IJEM journal page](https://journals.lww.com/indjem/pages/default.aspx)
- **ICMR-NIN review in *Obesity Pillars* (around Sep 2026):** diet plus exercise is the most effective approach to weight loss. Data from India were under-represented. — [NewsMeter](https://newsmeter.in/science-health/want-to-lose-weight-icmr-nin-study-says-healthy-eating-and-regular-exercise-work-best-together-775521)
- **Vitamin D or B12 in women:** I found no Indian study from the window on vitamin D or B12 deficiency in women. Search returned only 2018–2024 studies.

### Inferences
- Practical angle for ICMR-SALT-T 1: "Iodised salt worked, and goitre halved. But autoimmune thyroid disease (Hashimoto's-type, anti-TPO) is a separate issue that iodised salt does not prevent. If you have symptoms or a family history, ask for TSH plus anti-TPO. Don't drop iodised salt, and don't take high-dose iodine or kelp supplements on your own."
- Evidence strength: large national cross-sectional study, good for prevalence but cannot show causation; the population is children.

### Gaps
- I could not open the full Lancet text to check the exact anti-TPO figure (10.3% vs 10.4%), any girls-versus-boys split, or the household salt data.
- I could not search IJMR's Oct–Nov 2026 issues, because fetches were blocked and search surfaced nothing.
- I found no 2026 Indian peer-reviewed study on gut health in women.

## Q3. Is there credible evidence that use of non-iodised salt (pink or rock salt) is rising in India, and what does it say?

### Takeaway
**There is no credible population-level data showing that pink or rock salt use is rising in India, or that it is causing iodine deficiency.** That claim mostly comes from blogs. What *is* credible: a 2026 Indian lab study found that rock, pink and black salts have negligible to undetectable iodine, though brands vary. National iodised-salt coverage stays high, at about 94% of households in NFHS-5, but some states have pockets well below that.

### Cited Findings
- **Market salt testing, 2026** (*Current Research in Nutrition and Food Science*, special issue on sustainable nutrition 2026; exact month not visible):
  - Method: market salts tested by iodometric titration.
  - Result: non-iodised black, rock and pink salts showed "negligible to undetectable iodine levels"; some read 0 or near 0.
  - Variation: one rock-salt brand measured about 31 ppm.
  - Source: [Food & Nutrition Journal](https://www.foodandnutritionjournal.org/vol14nospl-issue-sustainable-nutrition-2026/from-salt-to-health-iodine-content-in-market-available-salts-and-its-significance/)
- **Iodine standards:** India requires at least 30 ppm iodine at production and at least 15 ppm at consumption. — [Food & Nutrition Journal](https://www.foodandnutritionjournal.org/vol14nospl-issue-sustainable-nutrition-2026/from-salt-to-health-iodine-content-in-market-available-salts-and-its-significance/); see also [FAS USDA on FSSAI iodine standards](https://www.fas.usda.gov/data/india-indias-fssai-notifies-final-standards-fortified-milk-powder-and-iodine)
- **Global trend:** the Iodine Global Network has a blog on the rising popularity of Himalayan pink salt. This is a global trend piece, not Indian data. — [IGN blog](https://ign.org/latest/blog/the-rising-popularity-of-himalayan-pink-salt/)
- **Not credible:** a widely circulated "India's new hypothyroidism crisis: pink salt" claim comes from a fitness-club blog and should NOT be cited. — [icfitnessclub blog (not credible)](https://www.icfitnessclub.com/blog/indias-new-endemic-hypothyroidism-how-the-shift-toward-pink-salt-is-creating-an-iodine-deficiency-crisis/)

### Inferences
- Safe, evidence-based framing: "Pink, rock or sendha salt contains little to no iodine. If it has replaced your regular salt, you may be missing your main iodine source. Use iodised salt for everyday cooking and keep rock salt for fasting days or garnish." Do not claim pink salt is causing a thyroid epidemic.
- **Seasonal hook:** Navratri and vrat fasting uses sendha namak.

### Gaps
- I found no Indian survey (NFHS, NIDDCP or market data) measuring how many households use non-iodised specialty salts or how that is changing.
- NFHS-6 iodised-salt figures were not found.

   - Source: [Medical Dialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/diabetes-endocrinology/news/iodine-deficiency-persists-in-rural-communities-despite-salt-iodization-suggests-study-177789)
