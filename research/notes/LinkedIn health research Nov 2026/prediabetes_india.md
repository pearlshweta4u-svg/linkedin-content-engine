## 1. ICMR-INDIAB national study (Anjana et al., Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol 2023)

### Takeaway
About 1 in 9 Indian adults has diabetes (11.4%, about 101 million people). About 1 in 7 has prediabetes (15.3%, about 136 million people). That makes prediabetes more common than diabetes nationally. Diabetes is about twice as common in urban India as in rural India, but prediabetes is not significantly different between urban and rural areas.

### Cited Findings
- Citation: Anjana RM et al. "Metabolic non-communicable disease health report of India: the ICMR-INDIAB national cross-sectional study (ICMR-INDIAB-17)". *Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol* 2023;11(7):474–489. Published online June 2023 (July 2023 print issue). DOI 10.1016/S2213-8587(23)00119-5. PMID 37301218. Evidence: **B** — [The Lancet D&E](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/landia/article/PIIS2213-8587(23)00119-5/fulltext); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37301218/)
- Weighted diabetes prevalence: "11·4% (95% CI 10·2–12·5; 10 151 of 107 119 individuals)" — [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37301218/)
- Weighted prediabetes prevalence: "15·3% (13·9–16·6; 15 496 of 107 119 individuals)" — [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37301218/)
- Estimated numbers: 101 million with diabetes and 136 million with prediabetes, derived using OGTT and WHO criteria — [The Lancet D&E](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/landia/article/PIIS2213-8587(23)00119-5/fulltext); also tabulated as 11.4% (101 million) and 15.3% (136 million) in [National Medical Journal of India, "Lessons learnt from the ICMR-INDIAB study"](https://nmji.in/lessons-learnt-from-the-icmrindiab-study/). Press used the same figures, e.g. [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/asia/india/india-study-estimates-11-of-population-or-101-million-is-diabetic-1.96302385) and [Scroll](https://scroll.in/latest/1050659/scroll_in).
- Sample: 113,043 individuals aged ≥20 years surveyed between 2008 and 2020, in all states/UTs. Glycaemic analyses used 107,119 individuals (the abstract denominator) — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37301218/); [Manipal research portal](https://researcher.manipal.edu/en/publications/metabolic-non-communicable-disease-health-report-of-india-the-icm/)
- Urban diabetes prevalence: 16.4% (4,272 of 31,560 urban individuals) — [The Lancet D&E](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/landia/article/PIIS2213-8587(23)00119-5/fulltext)
- Rural diabetes prevalence: 8.9% **[verify]**. This figure was seen only via a secondary ICMR-INDIAB Haryana paper citing the national data — [ResearchGate, ICMR-INDIAB-18 Haryana](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/380758224_Urban-Rural_Differences_in_the_Prevalence_of_Diabetes_Among_Adults_in_Haryana_India_The_ICMR-INDIAB_Study_ICMR-INDIAB-18). Another secondary review gave urban 17.2% / rural 9.4%, so secondary sources disagree. Use 16.4% vs 8.9% only after checking Lancet Table 2.
- Prediabetes did not differ significantly between urban and rural areas — [The Lancet D&E](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/landia/article/PIIS2213-8587(23)00119-5/fulltext)
- In many states with a lower Human Development Index, the ratio of diabetes to prediabetes was below 1. In other words, prediabetes outnumbers diabetes, which signals a coming wave — [The Lancet D&E](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/landia/article/PIIS2213-8587(23)00119-5/fulltext)
- Sex: the abstract lists "male sex" among the independent risk factors for diabetes (with age, obesity, hypertension and family history) in both urban and rural areas — [The Lancet D&E](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/landia/article/PIIS2213-8587(23)00119-5/fulltext)
- Do not mix with the earlier 15-state ICMR-INDIAB paper (Lancet D&E 2017), which reported 7.3% diabetes and 10.3% prediabetes — [Manipal portal](https://researcher.manipal.edu/en/publications/prevalence-of-diabetes-and-prediabetes-in-15-states-of-india-resu/)

### Inferences
- For a post about women, the safest headline is "136 million Indians have prediabetes (ICMR-INDIAB, 2023)". No women-only national prediabetes number was found in the abstract.
- The study defines prediabetes by WHO criteria (IFG fasting glucose 110–125 mg/dL and/or IGT). That is narrower than ADA's 100–125 mg/dL, so on ADA criteria the Indian prediabetes figure would likely be higher. This is an inference; the paper's exact definition should be checked in its Methods.
- Data collection ran 2008–2020, so "2023" is the publication year, not the measurement year.

### Gaps
- Sex-specific national prevalence of diabetes and prediabetes (women vs men) was not visible in any snippet. It may be in the paper's tables or appendix, which I could not fetch (thelancet.com full text and PubMed were not fetchable in this session).
- Exact rural diabetes % and urban/rural prediabetes % were not confirmed from the primary text.

## 2. Prediabetes cut-offs: ADA vs WHO vs Indian guidelines

### Takeaway
ADA defines prediabetes as HbA1c 5.7–6.4%, fasting plasma glucose 100–125 mg/dL, or 2-h OGTT glucose 140–199 mg/dL. WHO (and IDF) set impaired fasting glucose at 110–125 mg/dL and do not endorse an HbA1c prediabetes range. Indian RSSDI recommendations have used both fasting thresholds across editions.

### Cited Findings
- ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes—2026 was published as Supplement 1 to the January 2026 issue of *Diabetes Care* (vol. 49). The criteria are in Section 2, "Diagnosis and Classification of Diabetes" (DOI 10.2337/dc26-S002). Evidence: **A** (guideline) — [ADA professional education, SOC26](https://professionaleducation.diabetes.org/ProductInfo/SOC26); [Medical Dialogues summary](https://medicaldialogues.in/amp/diabetes-endocrinology/guidelines/american-diabetes-association-releases-2026-standards-of-care-major-updates-in-diabetes-management-announced-160346)
- ADA prediabetes ranges: A1C 5.7–6.4% (39–47 mmol/mol); FPG 100–125 mg/dL (5.6–6.9 mmol/L); 2-h PG during 75-g OGTT 140–199 mg/dL (7.8–11.0 mmol/L). Diabetes thresholds: A1C ≥6.5%, FPG ≥126, 2-h PG ≥200 mg/dL. **[verify against Section 2 Table 2.5 of the 2026 edition]**. The A1C and diabetes thresholds were confirmed via an aggregator citing ADA 2026 — [iatroX US guidance summary](https://www.iatrox.com/clinical-guidance/us/prediabetes-type-2-diabetes-screening-diagnosis). The FPG 100–125 range was confirmed via [Johns Hopkins Diabetes Guide](https://www.hopkinsguides.com/hopkins/view/Johns_Hopkins_Diabetes_Guide/547125/all/Prediabetes_or_Categories_of_Increased_Risk_for_Diabetes%C2%A0). These ranges have been unchanged in ADA Standards for many years.
- WHO/IDF impaired fasting glucose: 110–125 mg/dL (6.1–6.9 mmol/L); IGT 2-h 140–199 mg/dL — [Johns Hopkins Diabetes Guide](https://www.hopkinsguides.com/hopkins/view/Johns_Hopkins_Diabetes_Guide/547125/all/Prediabetes_or_Categories_of_Increased_Risk_for_Diabetes%C2%A0) (secondary; the primary source is WHO/IDF 2006 "Definition and diagnosis of diabetes mellitus and intermediate hyperglycaemia", not fetched)
- RSSDI 2017: prediabetes = IFG 100–125 mg/dL or IGT 140–199 mg/dL — [RSSDI 2017 PDF](https://www.rssdi.in/newwebsite/pdfdata/rssdiGuidelines/2017/RSSDI%20clinical%20practice%20recommendations%20for%20the%20management%20of%20type%202%20DM-2017.pdf)
- RSSDI-ESI 2020: HbA1c 5.7–6.4%, FPG 100–125 mg/dL, or 2-h PG 140–199 mg/dL — [RSSDI-ESI 2020 (IJEM, Ovid)](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/indjem/fulltext/10.4103/ijem.ijem_225_20~rssdi-esi-clinical-practice-recommendations-for-the)
- RSSDI 2022: reportedly lists IFG as 110–125 mg/dL (WHO-style), which differs from the 2017/2020 editions **[verify; seen only in a search snippet]** — [RSSDI 2022 (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/367148390_RSSDI_Clinical_Practice_Recommendations_for_the_Management_of_Type_2_Diabetes_Mellitus_2022)

### Inferences
- The same fasting glucose (e.g. 105 mg/dL) counts as "prediabetes" under ADA but "normal" under WHO. This is a useful, accurate point for the post.

### Gaps
- ICMR's own guideline (ICMR Guidelines for Management of Type 2 Diabetes, 2018) was not found in search results, so its prediabetes definition is unconfirmed.
- The ADA 2026 Section 2 text could not be fetched (diabetesjournals.org is blocked in this session). The ranges should be confirmed directly at diabetesjournals.org/care/issue/49/Supplement_1.

## 3. PCOS and type 2 diabetes (2023 International Evidence-based PCOS Guideline)

### Takeaway
The 2023 guideline (Teede et al., Monash-led, endorsed by 39 organisations) says women with PCOS have increased risk of IFG, IGT and type 2 diabetes. It names the 75-g OGTT as the most accurate test of glycaemic status in PCOS regardless of BMI, with reassessment every 1–3 years. Observational meta-analyses put the type 2 diabetes risk at roughly 3–4 times that of women without PCOS.

### Cited Findings
- Guideline: Teede HJ et al. "Recommendations from the 2023 International Evidence-based Guideline for the Assessment and Management of Polycystic Ovary Syndrome", published August 2023 simultaneously in *Fertil Steril*, *Hum Reprod*, *J Clin Endocrinol Metab* and *Eur J Endocrinol*. Evidence: **A** (guideline) — [Fertil Steril version, Cardiff ORCA PDF](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/161859/1/PIIS0015028223007197.pdf); [ASRM-hosted PDF](https://www.asrm.org/globalassets/_asrm/practice-guidance/practice-guidelines/pdf/recommendations_from_the_2023_int_evidence-based_guideline_on_pcos.pdf); [Flinders PDF](https://researchnow-admin.flinders.edu.au/ws/portalfiles/portal/150436661/Teede_Recommendations_P2023.pdf)
- Women with PCOS have increased risk of IFG, IGT and type 2 diabetes. Glycaemic status should be assessed at diagnosis in all adults and adolescents with PCOS, then reassessed every 1–3 years based on additional individual risk factors **[verify exact wording]** — [Cardiff ORCA PDF](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/161859/1/PIIS0015028223007197.pdf)
- The 75-g OGTT is the most accurate test to assess glycaemic status in PCOS, regardless of BMI. If an OGTT is not feasible, fasting plasma glucose and/or HbA1c could be considered — [ASRM PDF](https://www.asrm.org/globalassets/_asrm/practice-guidance/practice-guidelines/pdf/recommendations_from_the_2023_int_evidence-based_guideline_on_pcos.pdf)
- An OGTT should be offered to all women with PCOS when planning pregnancy or seeking fertility treatment. If not done preconception, it should be offered before 20 weeks' gestation, and all women with PCOS should be offered an OGTT at 24–28 weeks — [ASRM PDF](https://www.asrm.org/globalassets/_asrm/practice-guidance/practice-guidelines/pdf/recommendations_from_the_2023_int_evidence-based_guideline_on_pcos.pdf). Note: one snippet said "first prenatal visit" instead of "before 20 weeks", so check the wording.
- Reverse direction: women with type 1 and type 2 diabetes have increased risk of PCOS, and screening should be considered — [Nordic perspective, PMC10755126](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10755126/)
- Endorsement: the guideline is endorsed by 39 organisations — [Nordic perspective, PMC10755126](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10755126/)
- Risk magnitude (not from guideline text):
  - Danish nationwide cohort: HR 4.0 for type 2 diabetes in PCOS vs controls over a median of 11 years. BMI and fasting glucose were the strongest predictors. Evidence: **C** — [Rubin et al., JCEM 2017](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/102/10/3848/4096783)
  - Systematic review/meta-analysis: RR 3.45 (95% CI 2.95–4.05). Obese RR 4.86 vs non-obese RR 1.62. Evidence: **C** — [PMC10297688](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10297688/)
  - Earlier meta-analysis: OR about 4.4 (cited within the Danish study) — [JCEM 2017](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/102/10/3848/4096783)
  - Lay framing: "Risk of diabetes at early age quadrupled in women with PCOS" (Evidence **D**) — [Medscape](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/885421)

### Inferences
- "Women with PCOS have roughly 3–4x the risk of type 2 diabetes" is defensible if attributed to meta-analyses or cohorts, not to the guideline. Risk is much higher with obesity, but it is still raised in lean women.
- The key action point for the post: if you have PCOS, ask for an OGTT, not just a fasting sugar.

### Gaps
- I did not confirm whether the 2023 guideline text itself states a numeric risk ratio for type 2 diabetes. The full guideline (monash.edu) was not fetched.
- No India-specific PCOS-to-diabetes conversion data was gathered.

## 4. Prevention evidence: DPP/DPPOS (USA) and IDPP-1 (India)

### Takeaway
In people with prediabetes, structured lifestyle change cut diabetes incidence by 58% in the US DPP and about 28% in Indian adults with IGT (IDPP-1). Metformin cut it by 31% (DPP) and about 26% (IDPP). In India, combining the two added no extra benefit. The US benefit persisted, smaller, over 21 years.

### Cited Findings
- DPP: Knowler WC et al. "Reduction in the incidence of type 2 diabetes with lifestyle intervention or metformin." *N Engl J Med* 2002;346:393–403, published 7 Feb 2002. PMID 11832527. Evidence: **A** (RCT) — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11832527/); [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1370926/)
  - 3,234 non-diabetic persons with elevated fasting and post-load glucose; mean follow-up 2.8 years — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11832527/)
  - Incidence: 11.0, 7.8 and 4.8 cases per 100 person-years (placebo, metformin, lifestyle) — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11832527/)
  - Lifestyle reduced incidence by 58% (95% CI 48–66%) and metformin by 31% (95% CI 17–43%) vs placebo — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11832527/); [Maryland Dept of Health copy of the NEJM paper](https://health.maryland.gov/phpa/ccdpc/diabetes/documents/nejm%20reduction%20in%20diabetes%20incidence.pdf)
  - NNT over 3 years: 6.9 (lifestyle), 13.9 (metformin) — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11832527/)
- DPPOS 15-year follow-up: Diabetes Prevention Program Research Group, *Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol* 2015;3:866–875 **[verify journal and pages]**. Diabetes incidence was reduced by 27% (lifestyle, HR 0.73) and 18% (metformin, HR 0.82) vs placebo. Evidence: **A** (RCT follow-up) — [ResearchGate record of the 15-year paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/282132191_Long-term_effects_of_lifestyle_intervention_or_metformin_on_diabetes_development_and_microvascular_complications_over_15-year_follow-up_the_Diabetes_Prevention_Program_Outcomes_Study); [Medscape](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/826824)
- DPPOS 21-year follow-up: *Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol*, published online 28 Apr 2025. PMID 40311647. Lifestyle HR about 0.76 (about 24% lower incidence), metformin HR about 0.83 (about 17% lower) vs placebo. Median diabetes-free time was extended by 3.5 years (lifestyle) and 2.5 years (metformin). Evidence: **A** — [The Lancet D&E abstract](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/landia/article/PIIS2213-8587%2825%2900022-1/abstract); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40311647/); [GW Today](https://gwtoday.gwu.edu/node/28451); [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20250703/Long-term-study-confirms-lasting-benefits-of-lifestyle-changes-in-preventing-diabetes.aspx)
- NIDDK retrospective: "The Diabetes Prevention Program and Its Outcomes Study", *Diabetes Care* 2025;48(7):1101 — [Diabetes Care](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/48/7/1101/158195/The-Diabetes-Prevention-Program-and-Its-Outcomes)
- IDPP-1: Ramachandran A, Snehalatha C, Mary S, Mukesh B, Bhaskar AD, Vijay V. "The Indian Diabetes Prevention Programme shows that lifestyle modification and metformin prevent type 2 diabetes in Asian Indian subjects with impaired glucose tolerance (IDPP-1)." *Diabetologia* 2006;49(2):289–297. DOI 10.1007/s00125-005-0097-z. Evidence: **A** (RCT) — [Univ. of Helsinki research portal](https://researchportal.helsinki.fi/en/publications/the-indian-diabetes-prevention-programme-shows-that-lifestyle-mod/); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/7379354_The_Indian_Diabetes_Prevention_Programme_shows_that_lifestyle_modification_and_metformin_prevent_type_2_diabetes_in_Asian_Indian_subjects_with_impaired_glucose_tolerance_IDPP-1)
  - 3-year cumulative incidence: 55.0% control; 39.3% lifestyle (LSM); 40.5% metformin (MET); 39.5% LSM+MET — [ResearchGate IDPP-1](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/7379354_The_Indian_Diabetes_Prevention_Programme_shows_that_lifestyle_modification_and_metformin_prevent_type_2_diabetes_in_Asian_Indian_subjects_with_impaired_glucose_tolerance_IDPP-1)
  - Relative risk reductions vs control: 28.5% (LSM), 26.4% (MET), 28.2% (LSM+MET). These appear in a PubMed abstract of an IDPP follow-up analysis of subjects followed for 3 years (n = 502) — [PubMed 19587369](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19587369). They match my own arithmetic from the incidence figures (1 − 39.3/55 = 28.5%). A metformin review table lists IDPP n = 522 — [RxFiles evidence chart](https://www.rxfiles.ca/RxFiles/uploads/documents/Diabetes-DMT2-Prevention-Evidence-Chart.pdf). Sample-size figures differ between sources (randomised vs completed); verify in the Diabetologia abstract.
  - The paper notes an absolute reduction of 14.5 per 100 vs 7.2 per 100 in the US DPP — [ResearchGate IDPP-1](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/7379354_The_Indian_Diabetes_Prevention_Programme_shows_that_lifestyle_modification_and_metformin_prevent_type_2_diabetes_in_Asian_Indian_subjects_with_impaired_glucose_tolerance_IDPP-1)

### Inferences
- The Indian control group's 55% three-year conversion is a strong, accurate hook: without intervention, more than half of Indian adults with IGT in the trial developed diabetes within 3 years.
- The smaller relative effect in India (about 28% vs 58%) partly reflects a different, higher-risk population and intervention intensity. Avoid implying lifestyle "works less" in Indians; the absolute reduction was larger.

### Gaps
- IDPP-1 NNT values and the exact randomised n were not confirmed from the primary abstract.
- DPP and IDPP results by sex (women-only effect sizes) were not gathered. The DPP reports effects held across sexes ([Medscape](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/826824)).

## 5. World Diabetes Day 2026: theme and date

### Takeaway
**Correction to the brief:** the 2026 theme is NOT "Diabetes and well-being". The IDF theme for World Diabetes Day 2026–27 is **"Early action on diabetes"**, focused on early diagnosis and risk reduction, with the campaign tag #ActEarlyOnDiabetes. "Diabetes and well-being" was the 2024–2026 theme announced earlier, and some secondary sites still list it for 2026. The date is Saturday 14 November 2026.

### Cited Findings
- IDF: "World Diabetes Day 2026-27: Early action on diabetes". The campaign highlights early action to diagnose and reduce diabetes risk. It notes type 2 diabetes often has no early symptoms and recommends regular testing, especially after age 30 or earlier with risk factors. Evidence: **A** (official) — [IDF news, WDD 2026-27](https://idf.org/news-and-resources/news/wdd-2026-27/); [worlddiabetesday.org](https://worlddiabetesday.org/)
- Hashtags: #WorldDiabetesDay and #ActEarlyOnDiabetes — [IDF news](https://idf.org/news-and-resources/news/wdd-2026-27/)
- IDF online event on 10 November 2026 presenting new IDF research on type 2 diabetes risk awareness — [IDF](https://idf.org/news-and-resources/news/wdd-2026-27/); [IDF webinar page](https://idf.org/news-and-resources/webinars/world-diabetes-day-2026-how-to-get-involved/)
- WHO campaign page for World Diabetes Day 2026 emphasises earlier diagnosis, access to comprehensive care and reducing stigma — [WHO](https://www.who.int/campaigns/world-diabetes-day/2026)
- Date: 14 November 2026 (a Saturday) — [awarenessdays.com](https://www.awarenessdays.com/awareness-days-calendar/world-diabetes-day/)
- Conflicting/outdated: "Diabetes and Well-being" was described as the 2024–2026 theme — [Dagens Diabetes (Sweden)](https://dagensdiabetes.se/?p=4210). Treat this as superseded by IDF's own 2026–27 announcement.
- Background: WDD was created in 1991 by IDF and WHO and became an official UN day in 2006 — [IDF](https://idf.org/news-and-resources/news/wdd-2026-27/)

### Inferences
- "Early action" aligns well with a prediabetes-in-women post: prediabetes is the stage where early action works (see DPP/IDPP).

### Gaps
- I could not open the IDF page directly to quote its exact wording. The theme and tag come from search-indexed IDF pages, and two independent official domains (IDF and WHO) agree.

## 6. Optional: Indian women aged 30–50, perimenopause and glucose

### Takeaway
No national, women-only prediabetes figure for ages 30–50 was found. The best available women-specific data are from NFHS (random glucose, ages 15–49) and LASI (ages 45+), and they point to a rising burden in mid-life women.

### Cited Findings
- NFHS-4 district analysis: age-adjusted diabetes prevalence among women aged 35–49 in India was about 10.7%. Evidence: **B** — [PMC7270370, spatial variation of diabetes among women across 640 districts](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7270370/)
- NFHS-based (all adults, not sex-split): in ages 35–49, 6.63% had prediabetes and 9.19% had diabetes **[NFHS uses random capillary glucose, so it is not comparable with ICMR-INDIAB OGTT figures]** — [Epidemiology and Health 2020](https://www.e-epih.org/journal/view.php?doi=10.4178%2Fepih.e2020065)
- LASI (adults 45+): prediabetes (HbA1c 5.7–6.4%) was more common in women than men, with an adjusted prevalence ratio of 1.12 (1.06–1.19). The authors suggest post-menopausal weight gain, abdominal adiposity and lower physical activity as explanations. Evidence: **B/C**. This was seen in a search snippet, and the underlying paper URL was not isolated **[verify source]** — summarised in [Frontiers in Public Health 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2026.1766812/full)
- NFHS-5: premature menopause is rising in India, particularly at ages 30–39 — [PMC11303560](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11303560/)
- NFHS-5 women aged 15–49: determinants of diabetes — [Springer, Int J Diabetes Dev Ctries 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13410-023-01237-w)

### Inferences
- A careful claim: "In India, prediabetes appears to become more common in women than men after midlife (LASI, 45+)". Do not extend this to ages 30–44.

### Gaps
- No primary Indian study was found directly linking perimenopause to glucose change. International data (e.g. SWAN) were not searched in this session.
- Sex-specific ICMR-INDIAB prevalence remains the most valuable missing number. Check the Lancet paper's appendix.
