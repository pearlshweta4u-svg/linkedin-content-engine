## Q2. New studies on resistance training, protein (esp. vegetarian) or muscle/bone in midlife women in the window?

### Takeaway
One verified in-window item: a review in *Current Obstetrics and Gynecology Reports* (published 23 Nov 2026). It concludes that resistance training, not protein alone, has the most consistent evidence for improving muscle and abdominal fat in women going through menopause. I found no in-window vegetarian-protein study whose date I could verify.

### Cited Findings
- **VERIFIED, IN WINDOW:** Reyes H, Aggarwal B. "Changes in Body Composition During the Menopausal Transition and Cardiovascular Health Implications: A Contemporary Review." *Current Obstetrics and Gynecology Reports* (Springer) 15(1):49. Received 29 Sep 2026, accepted 16 Nov 2026, publication date in citation 23 Nov 2026. The review covers 16 primary studies from 2021-2026 (5 RCTs, 6 prospective cohorts, 5 cross-sectional). Findings in plain words:
  - Gain in belly fat, especially fat around the organs, is the body change most tied to menopause and is most linked to worse heart health.
  - Loss of muscle and bone happens too but is hard to separate from ageing.
  - Resistance training had the most consistent support and improved muscle and abdominal fat in several trials, though responses varied by menopause stage. The authors say this supports "resistance training as part of broader physical activity counseling for midlife women".
  - A high-protein diet without training did not change body composition. Training produced muscle gains with both high-protein and normal diets.
  - The review gives no dose for training or protein.
  - Evidence strength: moderate-low. It is a narrative or contemporary review, not a systematic review, built on small trials that mostly measured risk factors rather than heart events. — [PMC13789767](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13789767/)
- **Outside the window (5 May 2026), useful context:** a survey of 857 US women aged 45-55 (McLean et al., *Exercise, Sport, and Movement* 4(3):e00065). Resistance training was the activity most women wanted to do (74.0%). Top barriers were tiredness (68.5%) and lack of time (65.1%); the top facilitator was a structured plan (52.4%). The survey was not validated and the sample was mostly white, highly educated university staff. — [PMC13143370](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13143370/)
- **Vegetarian protein, date NOT verified:** IMPACT randomised trial in adults aged 55+ during resistance exercise. Both the vegetarian and omnivorous groups improved muscle mass, strength and function, but the vegetarian group ate less protein and the trial did not show non-inferiority for appendicular lean mass. This is from a snippet; the PubMed page could not be read (rate-limited). — [PubMed 42772454](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42772454)
- **Vegetarian women, 2026, likely outside the window:** after 16 weeks of resistance training, young strict-vegetarian and non-vegetarian women gained similar muscle and strength despite lower protein intake (about 1.0 vs 1.2 g/kg/day). The population is young women, not midlife. — [PMC12902596](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12902596/) (snippet only; date not checked)
- **Trial registration, not a result:** PLAN-RT (NCT07787533), a digital resistance-training programme for midlife women on GLP-1 drugs. The record was updated 13 Nov 2026; recruitment had not started, with an estimated start in Jan 2027. — [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07787533) (snippet only)
- **2026, date unknown:** Campbell et al., "Muscle and the Menopause", *The Obstetrician & Gynaecologist* 28(3):133-142. It says resistance training, adequate protein, creatine and vitamin D are more effective than HRT for maintaining muscle. — [Wiley](https://obgyn.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/tog.70046) (snippet only; date not verified)

### Inferences
- The Reyes & Aggarwal review (23 Nov 2026) is the one item I could verify that can anchor a post, framed as: "a new review says strength training, not protein powder alone, is what changes body composition around menopause."
- Vegetarian-protein claims should rest on established guidance and the knowledge files, not on unverified November studies.

### Gaps
- I found no verified in-window study specific to Indian or vegetarian midlife women.
- I found no in-window bone-density or osteoporosis study for women.
- The IMPACT trial's publication date was not verified.

## Q3. What practical advice follows, and does it support a save-worthy checklist?

### Takeaway
Only the strength-training message has an in-window primary source behind it, and that review gives no doses. A checklist is possible only if any numbers come from established guidelines or Shweta's knowledge files, not from these November items.

### Cited Findings
- Resistance training is the most consistently supported lever for muscle and belly fat in the menopausal transition. Protein alone did not change body composition, and training worked on both high-protein and normal diets. — [PMC13789767](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13789767/)
- Midlife women most often cite tiredness and lack of time as barriers, and a structured plan as the top facilitator. This supports a simple, scheduled routine. — [PMC13143370](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13143370/) (outside the window)
- Responses to prebiotic fibres vary between individuals. — [Nature Rev Microbiol](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41579-026-01383-y) (not verified)
- GERD lifestyle factors (weight, no smoking, activity, at most 2 caffeinated or fizzy drinks a day, prudent diet) were linked to 37% fewer symptoms. — [JAMA Intern Med 2021](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2774728) (outside the window)

### Inferences
- **Checklist candidate, strength:** (1) Put strength training on your calendar as fixed sessions. (2) Do training and eat enough protein together; protein alone will not do it. (3) Start where your energy is; a structured plan beats waiting for motivation. Any frequency, sets or protein grams must come from established guidance or the knowledge files, because the review gives none.
- **Gut checklist:** there is no in-window primary source strong enough. If Shweta wants a gut post, it should be framed as evergreen guidance rather than news.

### Gaps
- No in-window source gives numeric targets for training frequency or protein for women aged 30-50.
- Many publisher sites were blocked in this session, so in-window coverage is incomplete. Hosts to allow for a re-check: sciencedaily.com, nature.com, medscape.com, ecu.edu.au, news-medical.net, gastro.org, clinicaltrials.gov, europepmc.org.
