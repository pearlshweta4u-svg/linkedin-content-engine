## 1. What is India's salt iodization policy (NIDDCP, FSSAI rules, exemptions)?

### Takeaway
India has had a universal salt iodization policy since 1983. It runs through the NIDDCP and, since 2011, FSSAI Regulation 2.3.12, which allows only iodised "common salt" to be sold for direct human consumption. A 2019 FSSAI notification exempts traditional salts (rock salt, black salt, Himalayan pink salt) from mandatory iodisation. **A Madras High Court ruling, reportedly from August 2025, has quashed the mandatory-iodisation regulation. Its scope and whether it has been appealed are unconfirmed, so the post should not say "non-iodised salt is banned in India" until someone checks.**

### Cited Findings
- India adopted universal salt iodisation in 1983 so that all edible salt for human and animal consumption would be iodised. (Strong, but historical.) — [PMC review, via search extract](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3818611) [SNIPPET; older than 2023]
- The National Goitre Control Programme (started 1962) was renamed the National Iodine Deficiency Disorders Control Programme (NIDDCP) in August 1992 to cover the full range of iodine deficiency disorders. — [Vikaspedia, World Iodine Deficiency Day page](https://en.vikaspedia.in/health/health-campaigns/important-days/world-iodine-deficiency-day); [DGHS NIDDCP page](https://dghs.mohfw.gov.in/niddcp.php) [SNIPPET; page could not be fetched]
- Regulation 2.3.12 of the FSS (Prohibition and Restrictions on Sales) Regulations, 2011 bars the sale of common salt for direct human consumption, or as a food ingredient, unless it is iodised. FSSAI's FAQ says "edible common salt refers to iodised salt only". — [FSSAI FAQ on Iodised Salt, 28 Jul 2021](https://mail.fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/FAQ_Iodised_Salt_28_07_2021.pdf) [SNIPPET; older than 2023]
- The proviso to 2.3.12 allows non-iodised common salt for non-food uses. A 2017–18 draft amendment listed these as iodisation, iron fortification, animal use, medicine manufacture and industrial use. Whether the final text matches that list was not confirmed. — [FSSAI draft notification, 19 Apr 2018](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Draft_Notification_Restriction_Common_Salt_19_04_2018.pdf) [SNIPPET; older than 2023]
- A gazette notification of 25 June 2019 (published 4 Jul 2019) defined "common salt" for this rule. FSSAI's July 2019 release states: "traditional salts such as rock salt, black salt, Himalayan pink salt, etc. shall not be mandatorily iodised." — [FSSAI news release, 16 Jul 2019](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/media/FSSAI_News_Salt_FNB_16_07_2019.pdf); [Gazette Notification Common Salt 04-07-2019 (APEDA mirror)](https://agriexchange.apeda.gov.in/food_standards/ind_document/Gazette_Notification_Common_Salt_04_07_2019.pdf) [SNIPPET; older than 2023]. **Strong, and the key fact for the post: pink and rock salt are legally sold without iodine.**
- The iodine content standards for iodised salt are set in the FSS (Fortification of Foods) Regulations, 2018. A consolidated Prohibition & Restriction compendium (Version XI) is dated 1 Apr 2025. — [FSSAI compendium, 01 Apr 2025](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Comp_Prohibition%20and%20Restrcition%20of%20sales%20XI_01042025.pdf) [SNIPPET]
- History: in 2000 the central government lifted the ban on non-iodised salt, then reinstated it in 2005. — [Indian Thyroid Society Salt Iodisation flyer (undated)](https://www.indianthyroidsociety.in/images/ITS%20SALT%20IODISATION%20Flyer.pdf) [SNIPPET]; a [Tribune, 21 Jun 2000 letters page](https://www.tribuneindia.com/2000/20000621/mailbag.htm) shows the debate at the time [old]. A 2011 Supreme Court decision (Academy of Nutrition Improvement v Union of India, 4 Jul 2011) also touched on the compulsory iodisation policy. — [Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/665756/) [SNIPPET; old]
- **Recent legal challenge:** the Indian Thyroid Society flyer refers to "the recent Madras High Court ruling quashing the mandate for mandatory salt iodisation in India." — [Indian Thyroid Society flyer](https://www.indianthyroidsociety.in/images/ITS%20SALT%20IODISATION%20Flyer.pdf) [SNIPPET, undated]
- Beauty Without Cruelty India (an advocacy site) says that in **August 2025** the Madurai Bench of the Madras High Court struck down the key regulations mandating universal salt iodisation. It says the petition came from a Tuticorin salt traders' association and that the court directed the Centre to form a new expert committee with experts from both sides. — [BWC India, "Salt"](https://www.bwcindia.org/web/awareness/learnabout/Salt.html) [SNIPPET; Weak source]
- A Deccan Herald opinion piece, "Don't undo what we got right: why mandatory salt iodisation must stand", says the judgment rests on procedural issues, consumer choice and commercial freedom, and argues the mandate should stay. — [Deccan Herald opinion](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/opinion%2Fdon-t-undo-what-we-got-right-why-mandatory-salt-iodisation-must-stand-3712927) [SNIPPET; date not seen; opinion]
- Unrelated but recent: the Madras HC dismissed FSSAI's review petition against its February 2026 order requiring microplastics warning labels on plastic-packed salt, sugar and water. FSSAI and the Health Ministry withdrew a Supreme Court challenge in May 2026. — [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/madras-high-court-refuses-to-recall-order-to-disclose-microplastics-in-packaged-water-sugar-salt) [SNIPPET]

### Inferences
- Pink, rock and black salt sell legally in India without iodine because of the 2019 FSSAI carve-out. A woman who switches her main cooking salt to one of them loses the main dietary iodine source the national programme relies on.
- Three sources mention the Madras HC ruling: a medical society, a newspaper op-ed and an advocacy site. It probably happened, but the date (August 2025), its geographic or legal reach, and whether it was stayed or appealed are unverified. The safe wording is "India's iodised-salt rule has been challenged in court", or avoid saying "banned".

### Gaps
- The exact Madras HC case name, date, operative order, and whether the Supreme Court has stayed or overturned it. Indian Kanoon, LiveLaw and the court site could not be reached.
- The exact current NIDDCP standards (≥30 ppm at production, ≥15 ppm at consumer level are the long-standing figures) could not be checked on the DGHS page this session.
- No PIB release on the court ruling or on any 2026 policy change was found.

## 2. How many households use adequately iodised salt? Is coverage falling, is pink/rock salt use growing, is deficiency coming back?

### Takeaway
Most Indian households use *some* iodised salt (NFHS-5: 94.3%). The best national measure of *adequately* iodised salt (≥15 ppm), the India Iodine Survey 2018-19, found only 76.3%, and only about half of crystal and crushed salt samples were adequate. Women's median urinary iodine was in the adequate range nationally in 2018-19. No nationally representative data after 2019-21 show coverage falling or deficiency returning. Claims of a pink-salt-driven comeback are so far expert warnings and anecdote, not survey data.

### Cited Findings
- **NFHS-5 (2019–21):** 94.3% of households used iodised salt, against 93.0% in NFHS-4 (2015–16). The NFHS indicator is "households using iodized salt", based on a rapid test kit, so it is not the same as "adequately iodised". (Strong.) — [NFHS-5 India National Fact Sheet, MoHFW/IIPS via DHS Program](https://dhsprogram.com/pubs/pdf/OF43/India_National_Fact_Sheet.pdf); [NFHS-5 full report Vol II](https://dhsprogram.com/pubs/pdf/FR375/FR375_II.pdf) [SNIPPET; data 2019-21, older than 2023]
- NFHS-4 (2015–16): 96.5% of urban and 91.5% of rural households used iodised salt. — [Krishna et al., J Family Med Prim Care 2022;11(3):847–851, "Vast gap in iodization from production to plate"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9051721/) [FETCHED; 2022, older than 2023]
- **India Iodine Survey 2018-19** (Nutrition International with AIIMS New Delhi, ICCIDD/IGN and others; results released 9 Sep 2019): 21,406 households across all states and UTs; **76.3%** of households had adequately iodised salt (≥15 ppm). (Strong.) — [IIS 2018-19 final report PDF, Nutrition International](https://www.nutritionintl.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/India-Iodine-Survey-2018-19-final-report.pdf); [Nutrition International news release](https://nutritionintl.org/news/all-news/nutrition-international-and-partners-disseminate-results-of-india-iodine-survey-2018-19/) [SNIPPET; older than 2023]
- IIS 2018-19: 13 of 36 states/UTs had reached Universal Salt Iodisation (>90% of households). Jammu & Kashmir had the highest coverage and Tamil Nadu the lowest. Odisha had 65.8%. — same report; [Nutrition International release](https://nutritionintl.org/news/all-news/nutrition-international-and-partners-disseminate-results-of-india-iodine-survey-2018-19/); Odisha figure via [IJCMPH 2025 article](https://www.ijcmph.com/index.php/ijcmph/article/download/13733/8556/70351) [SNIPPET]
- IIS 2018-19, by salt type: about 83% of refined salt samples were adequately iodised, against **49.7% of crushed** and **42.5% of crystal** salt samples. — [IIS 2018-19 report](https://www.nutritionintl.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/India-Iodine-Survey-2018-19-final-report.pdf) [SNIPPET; older than 2023]. Useful for the post: loose and crystal salt is often under-iodised, not only pink salt.
- IIS 2018-19, women's iodine status: median urinary iodine concentration was **173.4 µg/L in pregnant women**, 172.8 µg/L in lactating women and 178.0 µg/L in non-pregnant, non-lactating women. All are in the adequate range nationally. (Strong.) — [IIS 2018-19 report](https://www.nutritionintl.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/India-Iodine-Survey-2018-19-final-report.pdf); also reported in Jha et al., J Nutr 2023 (PubMed 37390906, via [BVS record](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/portal/resource/es/mdl-37390906)) [SNIPPET]
- IIS 2018-19 awareness: 55% of respondents had heard of iodised salt, and 61.4% of those named goitre prevention as its main benefit. — [India TV News report](https://indiatvnews.com/lifestyle/news-76-percent-of-indian-households-consume-adequately-iodised-salt-548372); [Lokmat Times](https://www.lokmattimes.com/national/76-of-indian-households-consume-adequately-iodised-salt/amp) [SNIPPET; news reporting of survey]
- National Iodine and Salt Intake Survey 2014–15: adequately iodised salt coverage was only 62% in South India. Rajasthan, the second-largest producer, had 65.5% household coverage. — [Krishna et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9051721/) [FETCHED; older than 2023]
- Iodine is lost in storage and cooking. Salt bought more than 6 months after manufacture, or stored near the stove or in some containers, loses more iodine, and boiling caused about 40% iodine loss in one study. (Moderate; review of smaller studies.) — [Krishna et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9051721/) [FETCHED; older than 2023]
- Programme monitoring: the MoHFW NIDDCP page reportedly says that of salt samples tested by States/UTs in **April–December 2024**, 93.3% were adequately iodised by the volumetric (titration) method and 92.4% were of good quality by salt testing kits. — [DGHS NIDDCP page](https://dghs.mohfw.gov.in/niddcp.php) [SNIPPET only; page unreachable; verify]
- Long-run trend: household coverage of adequately iodised salt was under 5% in 1985, 51% in NFHS-3 (2005–06), 71% in CES 2009 and 93% in NFHS-4. — summarised in search extract of [DGHS NIDDCP page](https://dghs.mohfw.gov.in/niddcp.php) [SNIPPET; verify]
- **Pregnant women, recent (small) data:** in a hospital study in eastern India (70 mother–newborn pairs, Dec 2024–Apr 2025), 18.6% of mothers were iodine-deficient (UIC <150 µg/L) before delivery and 22.9% after. The authors note that a subset stays deficient despite overall sufficiency. (Weak–moderate: single site, n=70.) — [Int J Reprod Contracept Obstet Gynecol (IJRCOG)](https://www.ijrcog.org/index.php/ijrcog/article/view/16889) [SNIPPET]
- Older regional pregnancy data show pockets of insufficiency. In Uttarakhand (n=1,727), median UIC was 110–124 µg/L, below the 150 µg/L adequacy cut-off for pregnancy. — [PubMed 25593836 (2015)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25593836/) [SNIPPET; older than 2023]. In Kolkata, 37% of 237 pregnant women were insufficient. — [IJEM via Ovid](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/indjem/fulltext/10.4103/2230-8210.137491~prevalence-of-iodine-deficiency-among-pregnant-and-lactating) [SNIPPET; older than 2023]
- A 2025 global burden paper notes that nationally representative UIC data for India are lacking. — [Frontiers in Nutrition 2025, global burden of iodine deficiency](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1577169/pdf) [SNIPPET]
- **Pink/rock salt trend:** a Newsmeter piece for Global IDD Prevention Day (date unclear, around Oct 2025) says nutrition experts warn of "a quiet reversal". It says urban buyers are moving to pink Himalayan, rock, sea and boutique salts that often lack added iodine, and that mild deficiencies persist in several states despite over 90% household iodised salt use (citing NIDDCP). (Weak: news, unnamed experts, no data.) — [Newsmeter](https://newsmeter.in/science-health/global-iodine-deficiency-disorders-prevention-day-experts-flag-himalayan-salt-rock-salt-sea-salt-756838) [SNIPPET]
- A First Check (health fact-check site) article on a viral Instagram reel says experts warn that Himalayan and rock salt lack iodine, and quotes a dietitian saying deficiency cases are "resurfacing". This is presented as expert opinion, not survey data. (Weak.) — [First Check, "Is Iodine Deficiency Back? The Truth About Himalayan Salt"](https://firstcheck.in/iodine-deficiency-himalayan-salt-risk/) [SNIPPET]
- A fitness-club blog claims a "new hypothyroidism crisis" from pink salt but cites no study. **Do not use.** — [icfitnessclub.com blog](https://www.icfitnessclub.com/blog/indias-new-endemic-hypothyroidism-how-the-shift-toward-pink-salt-is-creating-an-iodine-deficiency-crisis/)

### Inferences
- The "94% vs 76%" gap is real and worth explaining in the post. Most homes have *some* iodine in their salt, but about 1 in 4 households (2018-19) had salt below the adequate level. Loose, crystal and crushed salt did worst.
- No national data after 2021 exist, so a post cannot honestly claim coverage is *falling* or that deficiency is *re-emerging* nationally. What it can say: pink, rock and black salt are legally exempt from iodisation, experts are flagging the urban switch, and small studies still find deficiency in some pregnant women.

### Gaps
- No national survey after NFHS-5 (2019–21) with salt iodine or urinary iodine data was found. NFHS-6 salt-iodine results were not found in this search.
- No market or sales data quantifying growth in pink or rock salt use in India were found.
- Women 30–50 specifically: no Indian dataset reports iodine status for this age group separately from women of reproductive age.

## 3. News and research from mid-2025 to November 2026 (including Global IDD Prevention Day, 21 Oct)

### Takeaway
The main recent research item is the ICMR-SALT-T 1 study in *The Lancet Regional Health – Southeast Asia* (2026). Among schoolchildren, iodine levels were adequate and goitre prevalence had more than halved since 2003, but 10.4% had thyroid antibodies and 13% had subclinical hypothyroidism. The authors back continued and strengthened universal salt iodisation. Coverage for 21 October 2026 found in this search was mostly exam-prep explainers repeating NIDDCP figures. No new official India data were released for the day as far as this search could tell.

### Cited Findings
- **ICMR-SALT-T 1** ("Iodine nutrition, thyroid function, and thyroid autoimmunity four decades after Universal Salt Iodisation in India: a cross-sectional study among schoolchildren"), *Lancet Regional Health – Southeast Asia*, 2026. It screened 8,903 children aged 10–18 in eight regions of India, January 2024 to December 2025. (Strong for children; not about adult women.) — [The Lancet Regional Health – Southeast Asia](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lansea/article/PIIS2772-3682(26)00151-4/fulltext) [SNIPPET; page returned 403]
  - Goitre fell from 23.5% (2003) to 10.3% (2024–25). — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/health/4-decades-of-salt-iodisation-cut-goitre-in-indian-kids-but-thyroid-autoimmunity-rising-lancet-study/3047879/); [The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/health/goitre-down-thyroid-autoimmunity-up-what-four-decades-of-iodised-salt-show-in-indian-children/) [SNIPPET]. One report gave a different baseline (17%), so check the paper.
  - Median urinary iodine was 133.2 µg/L, in the WHO "sufficient" range for children. — same sources [SNIPPET]
  - 13% had subclinical hypothyroidism and 10.4% were positive for anti-TPO antibodies (thyroid autoimmunity). — same sources [SNIPPET]
  - Authors' conclusion: "The sustained decline in goitre prevalence and absence of rising clinically significant thyroid dysfunction justify continued and strengthened USI implementation in India." — [Lancet RH-SEA abstract via search](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lansea/article/PIIS2772-3682(26)00151-4/fulltext) [SNIPPET]
  - Media framed it as "goitre down, autoimmunity up". The study is cross-sectional and cannot show that iodine caused the autoimmunity. — [Medical Dialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/mdtv/diabetes-endocrinology/videos/salt-iodisation-cuts-goitre-in-indian-children-but-thyroid-autoimmunity-is-rising-lancet-study-179731) [SNIPPET]
- Madras HC ruling against mandatory iodisation (reportedly Aug 2025) and the DH op-ed and Indian Thyroid Society responses: see Section 1.
- Global IDD Prevention Day 2026 coverage (21 Oct 2026) found only on current-affairs and exam sites. These repeat that 94.3% of households use iodised salt (up from 76% in NFHS-2, 1998–99) and describe NIDDCP. (Weak; secondary.) — [Adda247 Current Affairs, "World Iodine Deficiency Day 2026"](https://currentaffairs.adda247.com/world-iodine-deficiency-day-2026-causes-symptoms-prevention-and-indias-niddcp/); [Usthadian Academy, 24 Oct 2026](https://www.usthadian.com/world-iodine-deficiency-day-2026-and-indias-niddcp/) [SNIPPET]
- The most recent official GoI document found for the day is a PIB backgrounder from October 2024. — [PIB doc, Oct 2024](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2024/oct/doc20241020419801.pdf) [SNIPPET; contents not read]. AIR/News on Air covered the day on 21 Oct 2024. — [newsonair.gov.in](https://newsonair.gov.in/world-iodine-deficiency-day-aims-to-raise-awareness-about-role-of-iodine)
- A 2025 IJCMPH study on household iodised-salt use cites the IIS 2018-19 state figures. — [IJCMPH 2025](https://www.ijcmph.com/index.php/ijcmph/article/download/13733/8556/70351) [SNIPPET]

## 4. Strength of evidence summary (for the writer)

### Takeaway
Safe claims for the post: iodised salt is India's main iodine strategy; pink, rock and black salt are legally exempt from iodisation; about 1 in 4 households lacked adequately iodised salt in 2018-19; crystal and crushed salt are often under-iodised; and goitre in children has fallen sharply. Claims to avoid or soften: "deficiency is coming back nationally", "pink salt is causing a hypothyroidism epidemic", and "non-iodised salt is banned in India" (the last because of the court ruling).

### Cited Findings
- Strong: FSSAI 2.3.12 and the 2019 exemption for rock, black and pink salt — [FSSAI 2019 release](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/media/FSSAI_News_Salt_FNB_16_07_2019.pdf)
- Strong: IIS 2018-19 figures of 76.3% adequate and women's median UIC 172–178 µg/L — [IIS report](https://www.nutritionintl.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/India-Iodine-Survey-2018-19-final-report.pdf)
- Strong: NFHS-5 figure of 94.3% of households using iodised salt — [NFHS-5 fact sheet](https://dhsprogram.com/pubs/pdf/OF43/India_National_Fact_Sheet.pdf)
- Strong (children only): ICMR-SALT-T 1, goitre 23.5% to 10.3% — [Lancet RH-SEA](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lansea/article/PIIS2772-3682(26)00151-4/fulltext)
- Moderate (unverified on page): NIDDCP Apr–Dec 2024 testing, 93.3% of samples adequate — [DGHS](https://dghs.mohfw.gov.in/niddcp.php)
