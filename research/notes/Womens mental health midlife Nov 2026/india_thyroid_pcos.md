## Q1. India: NMHS 2015-16, treatment gap, NMHS-2, Lancet Psychiatry 2020 (GBD India), Tele-MANAS, Oct-Nov 2026 news

### Takeaway
In India's only national survey (NMHS 2015-16, 34,802 adults, 12 states), about 1 in 20 adults had had depression at some point. Women had higher lifetime depression (5.72% vs 4.75%) and higher adjusted odds of current depression (aOR 1.23). Current depression peaked in the 40-59 age band (3.6%) and was more than twice as common in large metros as in rural areas (5.17% vs 2.15%). About 80% of women with depression got no treatment. NMHS-2 results were due on 10 Oct 2026, but I could not confirm they were released.

### Cited Findings
**NMHS 2015-16, depression paper (CONFIRMED on PMC full text).** Arvind BA, Gururaj G, et al. "Prevalence and socioeconomic impact of depressive disorders in India: multisite population-based cross-sectional study." *BMJ Open* 2019;9(6):e027250, published 27 Jun 2019. Data from NMHS (NIMHANS), 12 states, 34,802 adults aged over 18, MINI interview with ICD-10 DCR diagnoses. [PMC6609075](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6609075/)
- Weighted current depressive disorder (DD): **2.68%** (95% CI 2.65-2.71). Lifetime DD: **5.25%** (95% CI 5.21-5.29). [PMC6609075](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6609075/)
- Current DD in **females: 3.0%** (n=18,217). The male current figure is shown only in Figure 1, not in the text. [PMC6609075](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6609075/)
- **Lifetime DD: females 5.72% vs males 4.75%.** Adjusted odds of current DD for females vs males: **1.23** (95% CI 1.04-1.45, p=0.016). [PMC6609075](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6609075/)
- Current DD was higher in females in every age group except 30-39. [PMC6609075](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6609075/)
- Current DD was highest in the **40-59 age group (3.6%)**. **Lifetime DD in the 40-49 group was 7.47%.** The paper gives no figures for women aged 40-49 specifically. [PMC6609075](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6609075/)
- **Urban metros (over 1 million people) vs rural:** current 5.17% vs 2.15%; lifetime 8.23% vs 4.48%. This is relevant to women in tech jobs, most of whom live in metros. [PMC6609075](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6609075/)
- **Treatment gap for depression: 79.1%** (females **80.1%**, males 75.9%). [PMC6609075](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6609075/)
- Disability among people with current DD (n=325): 78.15% had some disability (Table 3). Work life was affected in 67.28%, social life in 68.61% and family life in 70.15%. The abstract says "nearly two-thirds", which doesn't match Table 3. If you use the figure, cite Table 3 or say "about two-thirds". [PMC6609075](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6609075/)

## Q2. Thyroid autoimmunity / hypothyroidism and depression and anxiety; checking thyroid function in depression

### Takeaway
A 2018 JAMA Psychiatry meta-analysis found that autoimmune thyroiditis (Hashimoto's) was linked to about 3.5 times the odds of depression and 2.3 times the odds of anxiety disorders. The evidence is observational and the studies varied a lot. The authors advise testing for thyroid autoimmunity in people with depression or anxiety, and screening people with AIT for psychiatric symptoms.

### Cited Findings
- Siegmann EM, Müller HHO, Luecke C, Philipsen A, Kornhuber J, Grömer TW. "Association of Depression and Anxiety Disorders With Autoimmune Thyroiditis: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis." *JAMA Psychiatry* 2018;75(6):577-584, online **2 May 2018**, doi:10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2018.0190. A correction was published in *JAMA Psychiatry* 2019;76(8):872. [PMC6137529](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6137529/)
- PMC abstract: **19 studies, 21 independent samples, 36,174 participants**. Depression **OR 3.56 (95% CI 2.14-5.94; I²=92.1%)**. Anxiety disorders **OR 2.32 (95% CI 1.40-3.85; I²=89.8%)**. [PMC6137529](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6137529/)
- Conflicting figures: the JAMA Key Points (possibly the corrected version) report **20 studies, 22 samples, 44,388 participants, depression OR 3.31 (95% CI 2.00-5.48)** and an unchanged anxiety OR of 2.32. [SNIPPET ONLY, jamanetwork.com unreachable](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/2679767). The safe wording is "about 3 times the odds of depression and about 2 times the odds of anxiety".
- Moderators: study quality and the proportion of females did not change the estimate, but mean age did. Funnel-plot asymmetry pointed to possible publication bias for depression. [PMC6137529](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6137529/); [SNIPPET ONLY on funnel plot]
- Authors' conclusion: the analysis "establishes the association between AIT and depression and anxiety disorders". It may lead to early treatment of the organic disorder, "and not only psychotherapeutic treatment". The main text recommends testing for AIT in patients with depression and anxiety disorders, and screening AIT patients for psychiatric symptoms. [PMC6137529](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6137529/)
- Evidence strength: moderate for association (a meta-analysis of observational studies) and low for causation. Heterogeneity was very high (I² around 90%).
- NICE NG222 (Depression in adults, 2022): the quality standard QS8 assessment statement notes that depression symptoms may overlap with "physical health conditions, or menopause". [SNIPPET ONLY, NICE QS8](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/QS8/chapter/quality-statement-1-assessment). NICE CG91 covers depression with a chronic physical health problem. [NICE CG91](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg91)

### Inferences
- A safe line: "If you're low or anxious and also tired, gaining weight or losing hair, ask your doctor whether a thyroid test (TSH, and anti-TPO if relevant) makes sense." Frame it as an addition to mental health care, never a replacement for it. Never suggest stopping antidepressants or thyroid medication.

### Gaps
- I could not verify any explicit NICE recommendation to check thyroid function when assessing depression (nice.org.uk unreachable). Only a revision site listed TFTs, and that isn't citable. Don't claim "NICE says test thyroid" without checking.
- I didn't search for data on Hashimoto's prevalence in India or on whether thyroxine treatment improves mood.

## Q3. PCOS: 2023 guideline screening recommendations; prevalence meta-analysis (Cooney 2017)

### Takeaway
The 2023 International Evidence-based PCOS Guideline makes a strong recommendation, based on high-certainty evidence, that clinicians screen all adults with PCOS for depression and anxiety (and adolescents for depression). Cooney et al. 2017 found that women with PCOS had roughly 4 times the odds of moderate-to-severe depressive symptoms and 6.5 times the odds of moderate-to-severe anxiety symptoms.

### Cited Findings
- Teede HJ, Tay CT, Laven JSE, et al. on behalf of the International PCOS Network. "Recommendations From the 2023 International Evidence-based Guideline for the Assessment and Management of Polycystic Ovary Syndrome." *J Clin Endocrinol Metab* 2023;108(10):2447-2469, published **15 Aug 2023**, doi:10.1210/clinem/dgad463. It was also published in *Hum Reprod* (doi:10.1093/humrep/dead156), *Fertil Steril* and *Eur J Endocrinol*. Led by Monash, co-sponsored by ESHRE/ASRM/ES/ESE, NHMRC-approved, with 254 recommendations and practice points. [PMC10505534](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10505534/)
- **2.2.1 (EBR, strong ❖❖❖❖, high certainty ⊕⊕⊕⊕):** be aware of "the high prevalence of moderate to severe depressive symptoms and depression" in adults and adolescents, and "screen for depression in all adults and adolescents with PCOS" using "regionally validated screening tools". [PMC10505534](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10505534/)
- **2.2.2 (EBR, strong, high certainty):** "screen for anxiety in all adults with PCOS" using regionally validated tools. This applies to adults only. [PMC10505534](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10505534/)
- **2.2.3 (CR, strong):** if moderate or severe symptoms are found, assess further, refer or offer treatment. [PMC10505534](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10505534/)
- **2.2.4 (PP):** "The optimal interval for anxiety and depression screening is not known." A pragmatic approach is to screen at diagnosis and repeat based on "clinical judgement, risk factors, comorbidities, and life events". Screening should include risk of self-harm and suicidal intent. [PMC10505534](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10505534/)
- **2.7.1 (CR):** offer psychological therapy for diagnosed depression, anxiety or eating disorders. **2.8.1 (CR, conditional):** psychological therapy "could be considered first-line". Antidepressants are considered in adults when mental health disorders "are clearly documented and persistent" or when suicidal symptoms are present, following general-population guidelines. **2.8.2 (PP):** lifestyle changes and PCOS treatments (COCP, metformin, laser hair removal) may improve psychological symptoms. Use caution to avoid inappropriate prescribing and agents that worsen PCOS symptoms such as weight gain. Untreated anxiety and depression may affect adherence to PCOS treatment. [PMC10505534](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10505534/). This is not anti-medication guidance: it supports antidepressants where indicated.
- Cooney LG, Lee I, Sammel MD, Dokras A. "High prevalence of moderate and severe depressive and anxiety symptoms in polycystic ovary syndrome: a systematic review and meta-analysis." *Hum Reprod* 2017;32(5):1075-1091 (1 May 2017), PMID 28333286. [QxMD abstract](https://read.qxmd.com/read/28333286/high-prevalence-of-moderate-and-severe-depressive-and-anxiety-symptoms-in-polycystic-ovary-syndrome-a-systematic-review-and-meta-analysis) [SNIPPET ONLY: PubMed was blocked by a cookie wall]
  - Any depressive symptoms: **OR 3.78 (95% CI 3.03-4.72; 18 studies)**. Moderate/severe: **OR 4.18 (2.68-6.52; 11 studies)**. I²=22.4%.
  - Any anxiety symptoms: **OR 5.62 (3.22-9.80; 9 studies)**. Moderate/severe: **OR 6.55 (2.87-14.93; 5 studies)**. I²=59.6%.
  - All included studies were cross-sectional, so the direction of the association can't be established.
  - Prevalence of anxiety symptoms was about 43% and depressive symptoms about 37% in PCOS, vs about 10% in controls, as cited by a later review. [SNIPPET ONLY, unverified attribution](https://womensmentalhealth.org/posts/are-mood-and-anxiety-disorders-more-common-in-women-with-polycystic-ovarian-syndrome/)
- A separate pooled analysis, quoted in coverage of the 2023 guideline, gives lower ORs: depression 2.2 (1.69-2.86), anxiety 1.65 (1.15-2.38), eating disorders 1.53 (1.14-2.07). Guideline co-lead Helena Teede said 70-80% of women with PCOS experience these symptoms. That figure is commentary, not a pooled estimate. [SNIPPET ONLY, MJA InSight+](https://insightplus.mja.com.au/2024/37/new-guideline-for-polycystic-ovary-syndrome-management); [Healio](https://cm.healio.com/news/endocrinology/20241014/screening-referrals-needed-for-overlooked-third-pillar-of-pcos-psychosocial-symptoms)

### Inferences
- The guideline recommendation is the strongest citable point because it is verified, high-certainty and strong. Cooney's ORs show the size of the effect but are symptom-based, cross-sectional and unverified on PubMed. Prefer "around 4 times the odds of moderate-to-severe depressive symptoms" and avoid "causes".
- PCOS persists into the 30s and 40s, so the screening message suits the 30-50 audience.
