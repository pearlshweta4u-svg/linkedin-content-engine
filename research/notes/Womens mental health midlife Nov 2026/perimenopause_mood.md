## 1. Best meta-analytic numbers: depression risk in perimenopause vs premenopause

### Takeaway
The best recent pooled estimate (Badawy et al., J Affect Disord, 2024) puts the odds of depressive symptoms or a depression diagnosis about 40% higher in perimenopause than in premenopause (OR 1.40, 95% CI 1.21–1.61). It found no significant rise after menopause compared with before. Older SWAN cohort data agree that risk rises during the transition and that a past history of depression is the strongest predictor.

### Cited Findings
- **Badawy Y, Spector A, Li Z, Desai R. "The risk of depression in the menopausal stages: A systematic review and meta-analysis." *Journal of Affective Disorders* 2024;357:126–133. DOI 10.1016/j.jad.2024.04.041.** Online April 2024; UCL press release May 2024. [UCL Discovery PDF](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10192213/1/1-s2.0-S0165032724006438-main.pdf); [UCL news](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2024/may/women-are-40-more-likely-experience-depression-during-perimenopause) (both seen via search only; fetch blocked)
  - Perimenopausal vs premenopausal women: pooled **OR 1.40 (95% CI 1.21–1.61, p < .001)** for depressive symptoms and diagnoses. [HealthDay](https://www.healthday.com/healthpro-news/mental-health/risk-for-depressive-symptoms-increased-for-perimenopausal-women); [Physician's Weekly](https://www.physiciansweekly.com/risk-for-depressive-symptoms-increased-for-perimenopausal-women/)
  - Postmenopausal vs premenopausal: **no significant increase** in risk. [HealthDay](https://healthday.com/healthpro-news/mental-health/risk-for-depressive-symptoms-increased-for-perimenopausal-women)
  - Method: databases searched from inception to July 2023, random-effects models. **7 papers, 9,141 participants** in the meta-analyses. [UCL Discovery](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10192213/1/1-s2.0-S0165032724006438-main.pdf)
  - [SNIPPET, conflicting] Totals for the wider systematic review differ by source: "17 prospective cohort studies with 16,061 women" in some coverage, and 15,893 women (with 11,965 in the meta-analysis) in [Pharmacy Times](https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/women-during-perimenopause-are-more-likely-to-experience-depression-study-finds). The search summary said the abstract gives 9,141. **Use "around 40% higher odds" and the 9,141 figure only after checking the abstract.**
  - Limitations: studies used different definitions of menopausal stage and different depression measures. The authors could not account for prior depression history, and there were too few longitudinal studies to compare perimenopause with postmenopause. Their conclusion calls for early screening and treatment of depression in midlife women. [UCL Discovery](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10192213/1/1-s2.0-S0165032724006438-main.pdf); [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20240430/Perimenopausal-women-face-elevated-depression-risk-study-finds.aspx)
  - Press framing: "Women are 40% more likely to experience depression during the perimenopause." [UCL news headline](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2024/may/women-are-40-more-likely-experience-depression-during-perimenopause). Note that the 40% figure is an **odds ratio**, not a relative risk.
- **SWAN (Study of Women's Health Across the Nation), Bromberger et al. 2011, *Psychological Medicine*.** In 221 African American and Caucasian women (Pittsburgh site, aged 42–52, premenopausal at entry), the risk of **major depression** was higher during and immediately after the menopausal transition than in premenopause. A history of major depression was a strong predictor throughout. [PMC3584692](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3584692); [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/C1492885FCBF9B68C0990E70845F4DF4)
- [SNIPPET, secondary news only] SWAN depressive-symptom analysis (CES-D ≥16) in women without high baseline symptoms: **30% higher odds in early perimenopause, adjusted 73% higher in late perimenopause, 63% higher in postmenopause** vs premenopause. Risk was greater with significant hot flashes or night sweats, and independent of demographic, psychosocial and behavioural factors. [MDedge](https://mdedge.com/internalmedicinenews/article/19000/womens-health/risk-first-ever-depression-rises-during); [MDedge](https://mdedge.com/obgynnews/article/50921/gynecology/swan-new-onset-depression-perimenopause). This partly conflicts with Badawy (no significant postmenopausal rise); the two used different designs and outcomes.
- **India, 2021 meta-analysis (Asian Journal of Psychiatry, PubMed 33582315):** pooled prevalence of depression in peri- and **post**menopausal Indian women combined of **42.47% (95% CI 28.73–57.49; I² = 97.7%)**. Heterogeneity is very high. [PubMed mirror](https://api-pubmed-ncbi.laibokeji.com/33582315); [CAS catalogue record](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/cmedm,33582315)
- [SNIPPET] Indian single-site studies vary widely. Chandigarh: 200 urban peri/postmenopausal women aged 40–60, screened during COVID-19, had depression 39% and anxiety 29.5% ([J Mid-Life Health via DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/110d2afcad7f4427b4628688ed3bc444)). Rural Prayagraj: 320 perimenopausal women aged 40–55, assessed with PHQ-9 and GAD-7, had depression 84.69% and anxiety 82.50%, mostly mild ([IJCMPH](https://www.ijcmph.com/index.php/ijcmph/article/view/11418)). These are screening-scale prevalences, not diagnoses. **Do not use them as headline numbers.**

### Inferences
- A safe line for the post: "Large reviews suggest the odds of depression go up by roughly 40% during perimenopause compared with the years before it." Evidence strength is **moderate**: it is a meta-analysis, but of observational studies with high methodological heterogeneity and no control for prior depression.
- Badawy suggests the risk window is the **transition itself**, not "after menopause". That supports a message about watching for mood changes in the late 30s to 40s, when many women don't connect them to hormones.
- Indian prevalence data exist but are low quality and inconsistent. Best framed as "studies in India also find depression common in this age group" rather than quoting a percentage. If a number is used, the 42% pooled figure (peri + post combined, very high heterogeneity) is the most defensible.

### Gaps
- Could not open the Badawy abstract/full text to confirm the 9,141 vs 11,965 participant count or whether separate ORs for "symptoms" vs "diagnoses" were reported.
- Did not find a meta-analysis from 2025–2026 that supersedes Badawy 2024.
- The original SWAN 73%/63% figures were not verified in the primary paper (Bromberger et al. 2010, Arch Gen Psychiatry, is likely the source; not confirmed).

## 2. Who is at risk and what symptoms look like

### Takeaway
Credible sources agree that mood swings, irritability, low mood, anxiety and memory or concentration problems are common perimenopausal symptoms. Risk is higher with a past history of depression, past hormone-related mood problems (PMS/PMDD, postpartum depression), troublesome hot flushes and night sweats, and poor sleep.

### Cited Findings
- NHS: symptoms include mood swings, low mood or depression, anxiety, and problems with memory or concentration. They usually start months or years before periods stop (perimenopause), typically last around 7–9 years, sometimes longer, and can change over time. Symptoms can feel worse with sleep problems. [NHS – Menopause symptoms](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/menopause/symptoms/); [NHS – Menopause and perimenopause symptoms](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/menopause-and-perimenopause/symptoms/.)
- [SNIPPET] NHS-derived pages note that hot flushes may ease while low mood and anxiety take their place. [Cambridge University Hospitals NHS](https://www.cuh.nhs.uk/rosie-hospital/menopause-and-perimenopause/signs-and-symptoms/)
- Mayo Clinic: "Mood swings, irritability or increased risk of depression may happen during perimenopause". The cause may partly be sleep disruption from hot flashes. People with a history of PMS, premenstrual dysphoric disorder or postpartum depression are more likely to have perimenopausal mood changes. See a doctor if symptoms interfere with life or well-being. [Mayo Clinic – Perimenopause](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/perimenopause/symptoms-causes/syc-20354666) (the snippet garbled "dysphoric" as "dysmorphic")
- Prior depression is a major risk factor, but clinicians should stay alert in women with no history. (NAMS/NNDC 2018 guideline, via [NNDC](https://nndc.org/?p=8402); [MDedge](https://mdedge.com/content/guidelines-released-perimenopausal-depression))
- Vasomotor symptoms are associated with increased risk, duration, frequency and severity of both depressive and anxiety symptoms (systematic review, Gibson et al., *Int J Women's Health*, Feb 2025). [Dovepress](https://www.dovepress.com/article/download/100651) [SNIPPET]
- Anxiety burden: a Global Burden of Disease analysis (*BMC Women's Health*, Jan 2025) reported that the age-standardised DALY rate for anxiety disorders in perimenopausal women rose from 625.51 to 677.15 per 100,000 (1990–2021), with a projected 1,180.43 by 2035. [PMC11706191](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11706191/) [SNIPPET]. This is a modelled burden estimate, not a risk comparison.
- [SNIPPET] Vietnam, 2026 cross-sectional study (women 45–55): perimenopausal depression prevalence 25.1% (95% CI 21.9–28.6). Associated factors: domestic violence (POR 4.72), hot flashes (POR 2.04), painful intercourse (POR 2.02), chronic conditions (POR 1.85), worry about family problems (POR 1.68). [PMC13505764](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13505764/); [PubMed 42643903](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42643903/). This Asian sample may be relevant to Indian readers, but it is one cross-sectional study.

### Inferences
- "Brain fog" appears in the NHS wording as "problems with memory or concentration". That is the safer phrasing for a health post.
- Practical "who should watch out" list, supported by Mayo plus the guideline: past depression; past PMS/PMDD or postpartum depression; bad hot flushes or night sweats; poor sleep; life stressors. Evidence strength is moderate: consistent observational findings.
- Messaging point supported by the sources: mood symptoms overlap with depression and anxiety disorders, so they shouldn't be dismissed as "just hormones", and hormones shouldn't be assumed to be the only cause.

### Gaps
- No pooled estimate found for **anxiety** risk in perimenopause vs premenopause (no meta-analysis like Badawy's for anxiety).
- No credible Indian-specific data on irritability or brain fog.
- Suicide-risk data: the Menopause Society 2026 plenary covered it (see section 4), but I found no figures.

## 3. Current professional guidance on recognising and treating mood symptoms

### Takeaway
Guidelines agree on four points. A woman with mood symptoms in midlife should talk to a doctor. Clinical depression is treated with standard first-line treatments (antidepressants and psychotherapy). Hormone therapy can help mood mainly when other menopausal symptoms are present, but it is not a first-line or approved treatment for clinical depression. NICE (2024) adds CBT and HRT as options for low mood without a depression diagnosis that comes with menopausal symptoms.

### Cited Findings
- **NAMS + NNDC Women and Mood Disorders Task Group, "Guidelines for the evaluation and treatment of perimenopausal depression," *Menopause* 2018;25(10):1069–1085** (co-published in *J Women's Health*, Sept 2018; co-chaired by Pauline Maki; 11-member panel). [NNDC announcement](https://nndc.org/?p=8402); [Univ. of Iowa record](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Guidelines-for-the-evaluation-and-treatment/9984293654102771); [AMS-hosted PDF](https://menopause.org.au/images/docs/jwh.2018.27099.mensocrec.pdf); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/592602)
  - First-line treatments for perimenopausal major depression are **standard antidepressants and psychotherapy** (CBT etc.). [MDedge](https://mdedge.com/content/guidelines-released-perimenopausal-depression)
  - Estrogen has shown antidepressant effects in perimenopausal women, especially those with hot flashes, but it is **not FDA-approved** for perimenopausal depression. Evidence for estrogen plus progestogen is limited. [MDedge](https://mdedge.com/content/guidelines-released-perimenopausal-depression); [NNDC](https://nndc.org/?p=8402)
  - There is no menopause-specific depression scale; general screening tools such as the PHQ-9 are recommended. [NNDC](https://nndc.org/?p=8402) [SNIPPET]
  - Evidence strength: an expert-panel guideline based on a literature review, mostly observational evidence plus small RCTs of estradiol.
- **NICE NG23 "Menopause: identification and management", updated 7 Nov 2024.** The update added or revised recommendations on CBT and clarified HRT risk information. [NICE news](https://www.nice.org.uk/news/articles/updated-guideline-recommends-more-treatment-choices-for-menopause-symptoms); [NG23 update information](https://nice.org.uk/guidance/ng23/chapter/update-information); [NG23 recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng23/chapter/Recommendations)
  - Consider **HRT** to relieve low mood that started around the same time as other menopausal symptoms (depressive symptoms not meeting criteria for a depression diagnosis). [NG23 recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng23/chapter/Recommendations) [SNIPPET: wording paraphrased in search summary]
  - Consider **CBT** for depressive symptoms (not meeting depression criteria) associated with vasomotor symptoms; CBT is also listed for sleep problems. [NG23 recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng23/chapter/Recommendations) [SNIPPET]
  - [SNIPPET] The CBT evidence for low mood and anxiety rested on one moderate-quality study. NICE judged the data low quality overall but recommended CBT because it is safe with few adverse effects. [Red Whale summary](https://www.redwhale.co.uk/pearls/nice-guideline-on-menopause-2024-practice-changing-points) (clinical education provider, secondary)
  - Clinically diagnosed depression is managed under NICE's depression guidance (standard pathway). Not verified on page this session.
- **NHS:** HRT is described as very effective at relieving most perimenopausal and menopausal symptoms, and CBT (a talking therapy) can help with low mood and anxiety. See a GP if symptoms affect daily life, relationships or work. [NHS – symptoms](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/menopause/symptoms/) [SNIPPET]
  - Caution: one non-NHS factsheet in results claimed antidepressants "do not help with menopausal mood changes". The NICE line is that antidepressants should not be offered as **first-line for low mood that is due to menopause alone, without a depression diagnosis**. That is not the same as saying they don't work for depression. **Do not repeat the factsheet claim; it would discourage medication.**
- **Menopause Society 2022 Hormone Therapy Position Statement** (*Menopause* 2022, DOI 10.1097/GME.0000000000002028; [PubMed 35797481](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35797481/)): HT is the most effective treatment for vasomotor and genitourinary symptoms, with the most favourable benefit–risk profile for women under 60 or within 10 years of menopause onset. Treatment should be individualised and reviewed periodically. In women with bothersome symptoms, HT can improve sleep, mood and quality of life (secondary summary).
- [SNIPPET] MGH Center for Women's Mental Health primer: estradiol can improve mood and reduce anxiety, but menopausal HT "is not considered to be a first-line treatment for depression or anxiety" in perimenopausal women. It may be considered when antidepressant response is partial. [womensmentalhealth.org](https://womensmentalhealth.org/posts/menopausal-hormone-therapy-for-mental-health-providers/)
- [SNIPPET] Menopause Society 2025 meeting (Oct 2025), two systematic reviews (McMaster/Toronto): hormone therapy **does not consistently reduce anxiety** in midlife women (7 eligible studies); route, dose and baseline severity seemed to matter, with oral estrogen the most promising. Conference presentation, publication status not confirmed. [Menopause Society press release](https://menopause.org/press-releases/feeling-anxious-during-menopause-hormone-therapy-may-or-may-not-help); [Contemporary OB/GYN, 25 Oct 2025](https://www.contemporaryobgyn.net/view/estrogen-therapy-shows-mixed-mental-health-effects-in-menopause)

### Inferences
- A safe, guideline-aligned message: "If your mood has changed in your 40s, tell your doctor and mention your cycle and other symptoms. Effective help exists: talking therapies like CBT, antidepressants when depression is diagnosed, and for some women hormone therapy, decided with a doctor." No dosing, no product names, and nothing that sets HRT against antidepressants.
- Evidence strength: antidepressants and psychotherapy for diagnosed depression are **strong** (general depression evidence). HRT for low mood alongside menopausal symptoms is **moderate/limited** (small RCTs, guideline consensus). HRT for anxiety is **inconsistent**. CBT for menopausal low mood is **low-to-moderate** (NICE's own assessment).
- These are UK and US guidelines. Indian readers would see a gynaecologist, GP or psychiatrist; no Indian (FOGSI/IMS India) guidance was checked.

### Gaps
- Could not read the NICE recommendation text directly to quote exact wording or recommendation numbers (e.g. 1.4.x).
- Did not check Indian Menopause Society or FOGSI guidance on mood symptoms.
- No 2025–2026 update to the NAMS/NNDC 2018 perimenopausal depression guideline was found. It appears still current, but this is not confirmed.

## 4. Notable new items, Sep–Nov 2026

### Takeaway
The main recent event was The Menopause Society 2026 Annual Meeting (20–24 Oct 2026, San Diego). It had a plenary on mood disorders and suicide risk in the menopause transition and an abstract linking lower allopregnanolone to worse perimenopausal depression. Both are early-stage conference material, not practice-changing evidence.

### Cited Findings
- The Menopause Society 2026 Annual Meeting ran **20–24 Oct 2026, Hilton Bayfront, San Diego**. Its program lists Plenary 4, "Mind the Gap: Mood Disorders, Suicide Risk, and the Menopause Transition", and a 15-minute oral abstract, "Perimenopausal Depression Severity Is Inversely Correlated with Allopregnanolone". [Program PDF, Aug 2026](https://menopause.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/2026-TMS-Annual-Meeting-Program-15.pdf); [Meeting hub](https://hub.menopause.org/virtual-conference); [Contemporary OB/GYN interviews](https://www.contemporaryobgyn.net/view/top-video-interviews-from-the-menopause-society-2026-annual-meeting) [SNIPPET: titles only, no findings seen]
- [SNIPPET, unverified: surfaced once, not found on a second search] A Medscape report, "Lower Allopregnanolone Levels Linked to Greater Depression Symptoms in Perimenopause", described an observational study from the meeting. At peak depression (median MADRS 18), serum allopregnanolone was 72 pg/mL vs 187.5 pg/mL at the lowest-severity time point. The presenter suggested studying allopregnanolone-based treatments. [Medscape](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/lower-allopregnanolone-levels-linked-greater-depression-2026a10015qz). The related trials are mechanistic or early: brexanolone trial NCT05329779 was terminated with 2 enrolled ([ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05329779)); pregnenolone study NCT06238700 is recruiting ([CenterWatch](https://www.centerwatch.com/clinical-trials/listings/NCT06238700/study-on-allopregnanolone-and-depression-in-women-across-the-menopause-transition)).
- [SNIPPET] Flo app data presented at TMS 2026: higher physical activity was linked to lower overall perimenopause symptom burden (general symptoms, not depression specifically; app-based observational data). [Contemporary OB/GYN](https://www.contemporaryobgyn.net/view/liudmila-zhaunova-on-the-case-for-perimenopause-activity-trials)
- [SNIPPET] *Frontiers in Nutrition* review, 19 Oct 2026, on plant-derived nutrition and perimenopausal emotional health: evidence for supplements such as fenugreek and curcumin is mixed in quality. [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2026.1961915/full)
- [SNIPPET, preprint, not peer-reviewed] ALSPAC (UK) medRxiv preprint, Aug 2026: later age at menopause showed directionally consistent but small links with lower depression risk; the evidence was limited. [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.13.26360384v1)
