## What is new on perimenopause/PCOS/thyroid and mood, anxiety, stress or burnout in the window?

### Takeaway
Search results point to four candidate items in the window, all on perimenopause or menopause. Two are conference presentations from The Menopause Society 2026 Annual Meeting (San Diego, 20-24 Oct 2026), one is a retrospective study (Frontiers in Psychiatry, 16 Nov 2026) and one is a review (Bipolar Disorders, Nov 2026 issue). I found nothing in the window for PCOS, thyroid/Hashimoto's or insulin resistance. None of the four is strong evidence on its own. The best material for a checklist is that psychological symptoms of perimenopause are common and affect work, and that anxiety often comes with insomnia.

### Cited Findings

**Candidate 1: Psychological menopause symptoms and intention to leave work (Japan; conference presentation)**
- Publisher: The Menopause Society press release ("Psychological Symptoms May Drive Menopause-Related Workforce Attrition"), with news coverage dated 20 Oct 2026. The study was slated for presentation at the 2026 Annual Meeting, 20-24 Oct 2026. — [Menopause Society press release](https://menopause.org/press-releases/psychological-symptoms-may-drive-menopause-related-workforce-attrition); [News-Medical, 20 Oct 2026](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261020/Menopause-related-psychological-burdens-may-push-women-to-leave-jobs.aspx)
- Claim (from the search summary): in more than 1,600 perimenopausal and postmenopausal working women aged 45-60, the psychological subscale of the Menopause Rating Scale had the strongest independent link to turnover intention. Physical symptoms had a weaker link. The researchers called for accessible medical care and psychological support rather than only surface-level workplace adjustments. — [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261020/Menopause-related-psychological-burdens-may-push-women-to-leave-jobs.aspx)
- Study type and size: cross-sectional survey, n > 1,600 (exact n not verified). Conference abstract, not yet a peer-reviewed paper.
- Evidence strength: **early**. It is cross-sectional and measures intention to leave rather than actual exits. Earlier NAMS data found that only about 1% had actually quit or changed jobs because of symptoms in the prior 6 months. — [MDedge](https://mdedge.com/content/menopause-symptoms-negatively-affect-womens-work)
- Non-drug actions the source supports: access to psychological support and workplace support. It does not prescribe specific techniques.
- Note: this falls just outside the 30-50 age band (45-60).

**Candidate 2: Lower allopregnanolone linked to more depressive symptoms in perimenopause (conference presentation)**
- Publisher: Medscape news, 3 Nov 2026, reporting an observational study presented at The Menopause Society's October 2026 meeting. — [Medscape](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/lower-allopregnanolone-levels-linked-greater-depression-2026a10015qz)
- Claim: perimenopausal women had more depressive symptoms when their levels of allopregnanolone (a neurosteroid made from progesterone) were lower. The researchers suggested it could be a treatment target. — [Medscape](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/lower-allopregnanolone-levels-linked-greater-depression-2026a10015qz)
- Study type and size: observational. Sample size not available, because the page could not be opened.
- Evidence strength: **early**. It is mechanistic and associational, and it is a conference abstract. Related Brigham and Women's trials of drugs targeting this pathway are listed on trial registries (NCT05329779, NCT06238700). Those are drug trials, so they are out of scope for a coaching post. — [CenterWatch listing NCT06238700](https://www.centerwatch.com/clinical-trials/listings/NCT06238700/study-on-allopregnanolone-and-depression-in-women-across-the-menopause-transition)
- Use: it explains why mood can shift with hormone changes in perimenopause ("it's biology, not weakness"). It gives no practical action.

**Candidate 3: Anxiety in perimenopausal/menopausal women with insomnia (Frontiers in Psychiatry)**
- Publisher: Frontiers in Psychiatry, DOI 10.3389/fpsyt.2026.1990780. The search summary gives the publication date as 16 Nov 2026. — [Frontiers in Psychiatry](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2026.1990780/full)
- Claim: in a retrospective study of 384 women with insomnia, anxiety was present in 146 (38.0%). — [Frontiers in Psychiatry](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2026.1990780/full)
- Study type and size: retrospective, n = 384. The exact population, menopausal stage, risk factors identified and title were not verified.
- Evidence strength: **early** (single-centre retrospective design is likely, but not confirmed).
- Use: a sleep and anxiety overlap point ("if you can't sleep and feel wired, both deserve attention"). This overlaps with the sleep research stream.

**Candidate 4: Bipolar disorder and perimenopause, review (Bipolar Disorders)**
- Publisher: Radosavljevic et al., Bipolar Disorders, Nov 2026 issue, DOI 10.1111/bdi.70186, PMID 42811911. The exact online date was not verified. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/bdi.70186); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42811911)
- Claim (search summary): in people who already have bipolar disorder, more depression and more anxiety during perimenopause were the most consistent findings. Evidence on hypo/mania was mixed. Evidence on psychotherapy and medication, including hormone therapy, was limited or absent. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/bdi.70186)
- Study type: narrative/update review. Number of included studies unknown.
- Evidence strength: **moderate** for "depression and anxiety tend to worsen in perimenopause in people with bipolar disorder". **Early/absent** for treatments.
- Use: this is mostly a red-flag item. Anyone with a mood disorder history should tell their doctor when perimenopause symptoms start. It is niche for a general audience.

## Caveats, limitations and red flags

### Takeaway
All in-window items are early evidence: conference abstracts, a retrospective study and a review. Most mood-hormone research is cross-sectional, so it shows association, not cause.

### Cited Findings
- The workforce study measures intention to leave. Actual exits due to menopause symptoms were about 1% in earlier NAMS data. — [MDedge](https://mdedge.com/content/menopause-symptoms-negatively-affect-womens-work)
- The Finnish cohort did not show that the transition causes anxiety or depression. — [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261006/Women-entering-menopause-early-report-increased-anxiety-and-depression.aspx)
- PCOS: the 2026 meta-analysis found the anxiety link inconclusive, and some individual studies found no association. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13207832/)
- The bipolar review found little or no evidence on treatments in perimenopause. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/bdi.70186)

### Inferences
- "Talk to your doctor if..." red flags that these sources support:
  - you have a history of depression, anxiety or bipolar disorder and perimenopause symptoms are starting (Bipolar Disorders review)
  - your low mood, anxiety or irritability is affecting work or making you think about quitting (workforce study)
  - you have ongoing insomnia together with anxiety (Frontiers study)
  - you have any thoughts of self-harm. Seek help urgently. This is general safety practice, not taken from a specific in-window source.
- Never suggest stopping or avoiding prescribed medication. None of these sources supports that.

### Gaps
- None of the in-window sources gives specific, tested non-drug interventions such as exercise, CBT or mindfulness. A 2026 PCOS network meta-analysis (Tan et al., PeerJ) reportedly ranked emotion-focused therapy, peer support and mindfulness-based stress management highly, but I only saw it through a non-primary site, so it is unverified and its date is unknown.
