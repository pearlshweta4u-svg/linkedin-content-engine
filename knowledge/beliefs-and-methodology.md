# Beliefs, methodology and opinions

Rule (Shweta agreed, 9 Nov 2026): her clinical views are written as **her view or her experience** ("In my practice…", "What I see again and again…"). A number goes in a post only if it has a credible, cited source in the post record. If research contradicts a view, the post is flagged for her, not published.

Each item: the view → how strong the evidence is → how it may be written. Source for all: [ME] unless marked.

## Core model: the T4→T3 conversion environment
- View: thyroid medication (levothyroxine) supplies T4, which is mostly inactive. The body converts it to active T3. Conversion depends on cofactors: ferritin/iron, selenium, zinc, vitamin D, B12, and cortisol rhythm. If these are depleted, symptoms can persist even on a "correct" dose.
- Evidence: peripheral T4→T3 conversion via deiodinases is established. Selenium (deiodinases are selenoproteins) and iron (iron deficiency impairs thyroid hormone metabolism) are well supported. Zinc, vitamin D, B12 and cortisol have moderate or mixed evidence.
- How to write: confidently for the mechanism; "in my practice" for the six-cofactor checklist.

## Root cause before symptoms
- Root-cause sequencing comes before symptom treatment. Maximum **one new intervention per week**. Find the single lifestyle pattern causing the most downstream disruption and anchor the plan around it.
- How to write: freely. This is method, not a medical claim.

## Labs
- Functional/optimal ranges over standard reference ranges. "Normal on paper, wrecked inside." TSH alone is not enough: she wants free T3, free T4 and antibodies.
- Evidence: "TSH alone is not enough" is supported where symptoms persist or antibodies are relevant. "Optimal ranges" differ by lab and are often not guideline-backed.
- How to write: as her view. Never tell readers their doctor's range is wrong. Suggest they *ask* about fuller panels.

## Gut and inflammation
- Her view: most chronic inflammation she sees traces back to infections (parasitic, bacterial, viral, fungal), not diet alone. Diet can manage symptoms but won't resolve inflammation if an infection is untreated. Find what is overgrowing (e.g. H. pylori with stubborn acidity) before eliminating foods.
- Evidence: H. pylori and SIBO links to symptoms are established. "~90% of inflammation is infection" has **no supporting source**. Never state a percentage.
- How to write: "In my practice, when inflammation won't budge with diet alone, I look for an infection first."
- She has distanced herself from US functional-medicine influencer content. Don't imitate that genre (detox hype, supplement stacks).

## Hashimoto's
- View: many hypothyroid women never get antibodies tested.
- Evidence: Hashimoto's is the most common cause of hypothyroidism in iodine-sufficient regions. The "80% undiagnosed" figure is **unsourced**. Don't use it.

## Iodine and salt
- View: India's iodized salt was a public-health win. The switch to pink/Himalayan salt quietly removes iodine. She suggests using iodized salt alongside other salts.
- Evidence: universal salt iodization is established. Pink salt is not a reliable iodine source. Good material for Thursday/Friday posts with a cited source.

## Medication
- She respects medication and works alongside endocrinologists. Several clients want to "get off thyroid meds". Her position in practice: see the endo, agree a plan, and retest. Posts must **never** encourage stopping or avoiding prescribed medication.

## Supplements
- She suggests supplements in her practice. In posts: no brands, no doses, no "take X". At most, say a nutrient "is worth testing" and "talk to your doctor".

## Fat loss and training
- Strength-first; daily steps; protein; sustainable deficits ("Path A" over aggressive cuts); tracking that doesn't depend on streaks (a "winning week"). Cycle-aware adjustments.
- A natural bodybuilder's realism: she runs her own cuts with the same tools.

## Business beliefs
- The health issue has to be big enough for someone to invest in. (Hair-fall ads brought cheap bookings that rarely converted.)
- Honesty about results: "optimal on dose, not cured". She corrects herself when she gets something wrong.
